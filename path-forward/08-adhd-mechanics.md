# ADHD Mechanics (Not a Discipline Lecture)

You said you don't want discipline lectures. Good — discipline is the wrong tool for ADHD anyway. Discipline is a willpower strategy, and ADHD is precisely a condition where willpower-on-demand is unreliable. What works is **externalizing the structure** so that finishing doesn't depend on how you feel that day. These are mechanics, like gym form. Six of them.

## 1. WIP limit = 1

One active project at a time. Not one "main" project with two "background" projects — one. The others go into a `parked/` note with a one-line state description so your brain trusts they're not lost (this matters; ADHD brains keep unfinished things looping in working memory — writing the park-note is what releases the loop).

Your last three months are the control group for this experiment: 3 concurrent projects → 0 launches. Run the treatment: 1 project → launched → next.

## 2. Choose feedback-fast work — you already know this about yourself

You wrote: *"I am such a person who gets easily motivated when he sees some results."* That's not a flaw to manage, it's a design constraint to build around. It means:

- Ideas with weekly revenue/user feedback loops (services, B2B pilots, content) fit your brain.
- Ideas with 6-month silent build phases (platforms, deep tech) will die in your hands *regardless of their quality* — not because you're weak, because you're wired for short loops.

This is, conveniently, also the correct commercial strategy (profitable-from-day-one), so your neurology and your strategy point the same direction. Trust it.

## 3. Deadlines with witnesses

You hate imposed deadlines but you'll need them, so impose them yourself *publicly*. "AgentYap launches on X date" — posted on X/LinkedIn where people you respect will see it. External accountability substitutes for the executive function that ADHD taxes. Placement season is doing this for the TNP platform automatically (July 25 is a deadline with teeth that nobody had to nag you about — notice that it's the one project moving).

## 4. The 80% wall has a name — plan for it

The point where a project stops being novel and becomes finishing-work (edge cases, deployment, writing the launch post) is exactly where ADHD dopamine drops off and a shiny new idea appears. AgentYap was born at KD's 80% wall. This will happen again around week 2–3 of anything.

Countermeasure: when the new-idea itch hits, you get 30 minutes to write it into the idea bank — then back to the active project. The itch is real; the 30-minute pressure valve honors it without obeying it. And redefine "done" before you start: **done = a stranger can use it + you announced it**, not "code works on my machine."

## 5. Body doubling and environment

Working alongside others (even on different things) is one of the most reliably effective ADHD productivity mechanisms — this is half of why you crave "those kind of people" around you. Until you find them in person: coworking cafés, Discord co-working voice rooms, or even long focused sessions with me count. Your 6pm–11pm slot after the internship should have a fixed location and a fixed opening ritual, because ADHD task-switching costs (internship brain → founder brain) are steep and a ritual amortizes them.

## 6. Treat it medically too

You were just diagnosed. "ADHD is a founder superpower" is what people say *after* they've built management systems — often including medication, coaching, or therapy. Talk to your psychiatrist about the standard options. It is not cheating and it is not a personality change; every founder you admire with ADHD who talks about it publicly also talks about their management stack. The diagnosis is recent leverage — actually use it, don't just brand with it.

---

One reframe to close: you said you can't stand being instructed, and wondered if it's ego or comfort-seeking. There's a simpler explanation: **demand-avoidance is a common ADHD trait** — externally imposed tasks generate genuine (neurological, not moral) resistance, while self-chosen tasks unlock hyperfocus. That's why you "love grind" for your own things and stall on assigned ones. Knowing this, engineer around it: convert assigned work into chosen work by re-stating every task in your own words with your own reason before starting it. Sounds trivial; it's the standard trick, and it works. It will also make the internship and any future job dramatically less painful.
