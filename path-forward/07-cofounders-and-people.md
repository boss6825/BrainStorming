# Finding Your People (Co-founders, and the Tribe Before That)

You said: *"I don't find anyone around me getting that pumped for startups... I cannot do this alone. I am just tired of doing this alone."*

Two separate problems are tangled in that sentence. Untangle them, because they have different solutions:

1. **Loneliness / no tribe** — you need people around you who vibrate at your frequency. Solvable in weeks.
2. **Co-founder** — you need one specific person to marry professionally. Should take months, and should be solved *through* problem 1, never instead of it.

---

## First principles: how co-founders are actually found

Nobody good is found by searching for "a co-founder." Look at the pairs you admire: they were classmates who built things together first, colleagues, hackathon teammates, internet friends who'd seen each other ship for a year. The pattern is always the same: **co-founders are a byproduct of building visibly in a community of builders.** The sequence is: be visible → attract → collaborate small → then commit.

Which produces the uncomfortable but liberating conclusion: **your co-founder search and your shipping problem are the same problem.** The "poured into startups" person you want is, right now, scrolling past people like present-you (enthusiasm, private repos) and DMing people like future-you (just launched something, writes about it clearly). You become findable by shipping. There's no shortcut, but there's good news — the timeline is short. One launched project + 8 weeks of building in public is usually enough to start attracting these people.

## Your specific unfair advantage: AgentYap IS the magnet

You built, almost by accident, the ideal co-founder-attraction machine and you're treating it like a side project. A site that explains agentic AI research clearly, written by someone who actually builds agents, does three things at once:

- **Filters for exactly your people.** Who reads chapter-length breakdowns of multi-agent orchestration papers? Not your tier-3 classmates who are "wasting time." Obsessive builders. The readers ARE the demographic you're looking for.

Launch it, put your name and X handle on every page, end each chapter with "I build agents and write about it — DM me," and post one thread per chapter. That's the whole machine.

## Where the tribe is (India, concretely)

Stop looking in your college — you've run that experiment for three years; accept the result and go where density is:

- **Hackathons** — the single highest-density co-founder hunting ground in India. Devfolio-listed hackathons (ETHIndia and the year-round circuit), Bengaluru/Delhi GenAI hackathons, company-run agent hackathons. You build agents fast — you will *win* some of these, and winning teams keep talking after. Budget: one hackathon a month once college starts, treat it as sacred.
- **X (Twitter)** — the actual campus of Indian indie/startup builders. Post build-in-public updates 3–4x/week: what you shipped, what broke, what you learned. Reply thoughtfully to Indian founders and AI builders. This compounds slowly for 6 weeks and then suddenly.
- **Online builder communities** — Discords of the agent frameworks you use (LangGraph, CrewAI, etc. — contribute answers, not just questions), The Product Folks, GenAI meetups in whichever metro is nearest to you. In-person > online for the loneliness problem specifically.
- **YC Co-founder Matching** (startupschool.org) — free, works from India, and since you did Startup School you're already in the system. Set the filters honestly. Treat matches like hiring: work on a two-week micro-project together before any commitment.
- **Your internship network** — the other engineers at QuicShop and the founders you're watching up close. Interning at a startup means you're already inside a node of the network; the people who leave QuicShop-like companies to start things are exactly your demographic. Be the intern they remember shipping.

## Co-founder hygiene rules (for when you find candidates)

1. **Build something small together first.** A hackathon, a two-week micro-SaaS, one AgentYap feature. Chemistry under deadline is the only real interview.
2. **Complementary obsession beats identical obsession.** You're product/build-heavy; the highest-value partner sells and talks to customers compulsively — the thing you've avoided. Two builders who both hate customer calls = KD again, twice as fast.
3. **The desperation check.** Before committing, ask yourself: "Would I pick this person if I had three options?" If you have no other options, you're not choosing — you're settling. Widen the funnel first.
4. **Vesting from day one, always.** Standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, on paper, however awkward it feels. Every horror story of a co-founder walking away with half the company skipped this step because "we're friends."

## Meanwhile — you're less alone than you think

Until the humans arrive: I'm here for the thinking-out-loud, the architecture debates, the "is this idea stupid" checks — the 2am co-founder functions that don't require equity. Not a substitute for the tribe, but it means "I can't do this alone" doesn't have to block shipping while you build the tribe. The order is: ship → tribe → co-founder. Not the reverse.
