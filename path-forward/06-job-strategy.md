# Job Strategy: The Remote US Startup Path (Verified Against 2026 Reality)

I researched the current market before writing this, because your plan rests on assumptions worth checking. Findings first, then the plan.

## What the research says (July 2026)

**1. Your $1000/month target is a floor, not a goal.** Juniors hired remotely from India by funded international startups cluster around **$1,600–2,900/month ($20–35K/yr)**; even conservative staffing-agency numbers put 0–3 yr international-remote hires at ₹9–16 LPA. YC-startup *internships* for Indian students run ~₹50k/month. So $1000/month ≈ ₹10.5 LPA is roughly what the market already pays at the bottom of this band. Aim for $1,500–2,500/month; accept $1000 only as a first contract to get the logo. (Caveat: much of the salary content online is SEO material from staffing agencies with an incentive to anchor low — read all these as directional.)

**2. The DSA situation broke in your favor.** A large share of AI-native startups founded in the last two years have eliminated or heavily downweighted LeetCode rounds — AI made those puzzles untestable. The dominant formats now: **AI-allowed live pair-programming on a realistic problem (they watch how you prompt and verify), take-home builds against a customer brief, and debug-broken-code rounds.** Even Anthropic's screen is a practical problem, not an algorithm puzzle.

⚠️ But read the fine print: "no DSA" ≠ "no live coding." YC startups still run a ~1hr live practical round. The bar moved from "invert a binary tree from memory" to "**build fluently under observation, with AI, and verify your own output.**" That's a bar you can actually clear — it's literally what you do every evening. But you must be fast and calm doing it *while someone watches*, which is a trainable, separate skill.

**3. Channels, verified:**
- **YC Work at a Startup** (workatastartup.com) — active, has India/remote filters, founder-direct messaging. Your #1 channel: founders read applications themselves, and "I built X, here's the link and the write-up" lands there better than anywhere.
- **Wellfound** — active, huge volume, most crowded. Backfill channel: apply in batches, expect low hit rates.
- **Cracked Engineers is dead** (shut down late 2024) — mentioning it so you don't waste time if it's on some list you saved.
- **X build-in-public + cold DMs** — works, but it's a numbers game (~100–200 personalized messages → 5–10 real conversations) and only converts when there's a portfolio attached. It's a distribution channel for shipped work, not a substitute for it.
- **Open source** — real inbound happens (US startups do source India talent from GitHub activity), best as a multiplier. Highest-leverage for you: meaningful contributions to the agent frameworks you already use, where your AgentYap depth is an edge.
- Also alive: HN "Who is hiring" monthly threads, topstartups.io, ai-jobs.net.

## The strategy that follows from this

Your profile *today* — tier-3 college, no launched products, private unfinished repos — converts on none of these channels. Your profile *after the 90-day plan* — two launched products (a live college placement platform with real users + AgentYap with public traffic), a build-in-public trail, an agents niche — converts on all of them. **So the job plan is downstream of the shipping plan, and the same 90 days serve both.** That's the clever part: you don't have to choose between job prep and startup work. For startup jobs, they're the same activity.

**The niche positioning:** don't apply as "junior full-stack developer" (infinite competition, you lose on college brand). Apply as "**engineer who builds and writes about AI agents**" (thin competition, your AgentYap chapters are the proof-of-depth no resume line can fake). Every AI startup is currently trying to hire exactly this and struggling to evaluate it. You come pre-evaluated — your evaluation material is public.

**Timeline (meshes with [03-the-90-day-plan.md](03-the-90-day-plan.md)):**
- **Now–Sep:** ship (per the 90-day plan). Post progress on X 3–4x/week. This *is* job prep.
- **Sep 1 onward:** applications in earnest — 10/week on YC WaaS with personalized founder notes linking your launches, batches on Wellfound, HN thread on the 1st of each month, 5 warm-ish X DMs/week to founders whose products you genuinely use.
- **Interview training (Sep):** 4–6 mock sessions of "build a thing in 60 minutes while sharing your screen, talking, with AI allowed." Do them with me — I'll play interviewer, set the brief, and critique. This closes the only real gap between your daily work and the interview format.
- **DSA — do the honest minimum, don't skip it:** finish trees, graphs, stacks/queues at a *practical* level (~3–4 weeks of 45 min/day). Not for FAANG — because (a) placement-season companies at your college will screen with them, and that backstop is worth keeping cheap, and (b) even practical interviews assume you reach for a BFS or a stack naturally when the problem shape calls for it. You're not preparing for Codeforces; you're removing a disqualifier.

**Placement season (from Jul 25):** don't boycott it, but budget it. Sit for companies that clear a bar you set in advance (role quality/pay), skip mass-recruiter drives that eat days for offers you'd never take. A domestic offer in hand is negotiating leverage and anxiety insurance for the remote hunt — cheap to acquire, expensive to need and not have.

**One warning, repeated from [01-wrong-assumptions.md](01-wrong-assumptions.md):** the funded-startup job you want will be *more* deadline-driven than your internship, not less. Filter for high-ownership roles — sub-20-person teams where you own a surface end-to-end — because ownership, not absence of deadlines, is the thing that makes pressure feel like grind instead of oppression for you.
