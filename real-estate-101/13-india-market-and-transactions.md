# 13 · Indian Real Estate — Market Structure and Transactions

> This chapter and the next cover your home market. This one is the structural half: how
> the Indian market is shaped, why land title works fundamentally differently from the
> US, how money moves through a transaction, and what the law requires. Chapter 14 is
> the people half — who works here, what their days look like, and what they cost.
>
> **The single most important thing to understand before reading on:** India does not
> have guaranteed title, does not have an MLS, and its dominant transaction is the sale
> of an unbuilt apartment by a developer. Each of those three facts breaks an assumption
> that US-centric real estate software is built on, and each creates work that does not
> exist in the US at all.

## 1. The shape of the market

### The structural differences that matter most

| Dimension | United States | India |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant transaction** | Resale of existing homes | **Primary sales** — new and under-construction units sold by developers |
| **Listings data** | ~500 MLSs; fragmented but structured and licensable | **No MLS.** Portals hold duplicated, often stale, unverified listings |
| **Title** | Guaranteed by title insurance | **Presumptive.** Registration records a transaction, not ownership validity |
| **Who represents the buyer** | A buyer's agent with fiduciary duty | Usually nobody. The channel partner is paid by the developer |
| **Financing** | ~90%+ of purchases financed | Financing is common but a large share of transactions involve substantial own funds |
| **Transaction cost** | ~1-3% (transfer tax varies) plus commission | **Stamp duty and registration alone are typically 5-8%** |
| **Sale process length** | 30-60 days to close | Resale: 1-3 months. **Under-construction: 3-5 years to possession** |
| **Deal medium** | Email, e-sign, MLS, transaction platforms | **WhatsApp, phone calls, PDFs, physical visits, wet signatures** |

### The market's structure

Real estate and construction together form one of India's largest economic sectors, and
**construction is generally cited as India's second-largest employer after agriculture**
— a figure worth verifying against current government data, but directionally reliable
and important: this is a sector defined by enormous, largely informal labour.

Activity concentrates in the **top eight cities** — Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR),
National Capital Region (NCR), Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad —
with tier-2 and tier-3 cities growing. Alongside residential sit commercial office
(driven heavily by Global Capability Centres — see chapter 14), retail, warehousing and
logistics (a strong post-2020 sector), hospitality, and data centres.

### The shocks that reshaped the industry after 2016

Understanding the modern Indian market requires understanding four disruptions in quick
succession:

1. **Demonetisation (November 2016)** — withdrawal of high-value currency notes. Real
   estate, particularly resale and land, had historically carried a significant cash
   component. Demonetisation compressed it and hit resale and land values hardest.
2. **RERA (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, in force from May 2017)**
   — imposed registration of projects and agents, escrow requirements, mandatory
   disclosure, and an adjudication mechanism. It professionalised the sector and
   eliminated many small developers who could not comply.
3. **GST (2017)** — replaced a patchwork of indirect taxes; the treatment of
   under-construction versus completed property became a major determinant of buyer
   behaviour.
4. **IBC and developer insolvencies** — the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code exposed
   several very large developers. **Amrapali, Jaypee Infratech, and Unitech** became
   national cautionary tales, with tens of thousands of homebuyers left with unfinished
   flats and years of litigation. Homebuyers were subsequently recognised as financial
   creditors under the IBC.

**The net effect was consolidation.** Buyers fled to developers they believed would
actually deliver, which shifted market share sharply toward large, listed, well-capitalised
players — **DLF, Godrej Properties, Macrotech (Lodha), Prestige Estates, Brigade, Sobha,
Oberoi Realty, Puravankara, Signature Global** and similar.

**Why this matters to you:** the buyer's central anxiety in Indian real estate is not
"am I paying the right price" but **"will this actually get built, and is the title
clean"**. That is a fundamentally different problem from the US market's, and it points
at fundamentally different products.

## 2. Land, title, and records — the central structural problem

**This is the most important section in the chapter.** Everything difficult about Indian
real estate traces back to it.

### Presumptive versus conclusive title

India operates a system of **presumptive title**. Registering a sale deed under the
**Registration Act, 1908** records that a transaction occurred between two parties. It
does **not** guarantee that the seller had good title to convey. If the seller's own
ownership was defective, the buyer inherits the defect.

The practical consequence: a buyer (and their bank) must **investigate the chain of
title themselves**, typically by having an advocate examine records going back
**30 years** — the mother deed, every subsequent conveyance, and an **Encumbrance
Certificate** showing registered charges over that period.

Compare this to the US, where a title insurance company performs the search once and
then **insures** the result, absorbing the risk (chapter 04). India has no equivalent
functioning market. RERA contemplated title insurance, and products have been notified,
but adoption has been minimal — insurers cannot price a risk on records this incomplete,
and buyers are unwilling to pay a premium for a concept they do not know.

**This single structural gap generates an enormous amount of manual work**: every
transaction requires an independent lawyer to repeat a search of the same public records,
and every bank repeats it again for its own loan.

### The records themselves

Land records in India are maintained separately by different departments and are **not
integrated** with one another. A typical property involves at least three distinct record
systems:

| Record type | Maintained by | What it proves | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Registration records** (sale deeds) | Sub-Registrar, Registration Department | That a transaction was registered | That the seller owned the property |
| **Record of Rights / revenue records** | Revenue Department | Who the revenue department considers the holder, for tax purposes | Legal ownership |
| **Survey and settlement records** | Survey Department | Boundaries and measurements | Ownership |
| **Municipal records** (khata, property tax) | Municipal body | That the property is on the municipal roll for taxation | Ownership |

**They can and do disagree with each other.** Reconciling them is much of what a
property lawyer does.

### The state-specific vocabulary

Land is a state subject, so every state has different documents and different names for
similar things. A selection you will encounter:

| Term | State / region | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **7/12 extract (Satbara)** | Maharashtra | Record of rights for agricultural land: survey number, area, holder, crops, encumbrances |
| **Khata (A khata / B khata)** | Bengaluru / BBMP | Municipal account for property tax. **A khata** = fully compliant property; **B khata** = recorded but with irregularities (often unapproved layouts or missing approvals). B khata properties face difficulty getting loans and building approvals — a large, live problem in Bengaluru |
| **Patta / Chitta / Adangal** | Tamil Nadu | Record of title/possession; land classification and cultivation records |
| **Dharani** | Telangana | The state's integrated land records and registration portal |
| **Bhulekh** | Uttar Pradesh and several states | Online record-of-rights portal |
| **Bhoomi** | Karnataka | Digitised land records for rural land (RTC / pahani) |
| **Kaveri** | Karnataka | Registration department portal |
| **IGRS / IGR** | Many states | Inspector General of Registration systems for registration and stamp duty |
| **Mutation (dakhil kharij)** | All | Updating the revenue/municipal record to reflect a new owner **after** registration. A separate step people frequently forget |
| **Encumbrance Certificate (EC)** | All | A certificate listing registered transactions on a property over a requested period |

### Additional complications specific to India

- **Agricultural land conversion.** Agricultural land generally cannot be used for
  non-agricultural purposes without a conversion order (NA order / DC conversion).
  In several states, non-agriculturists face restrictions on buying agricultural land
  at all.
- **Gram panchayat versus municipal approvals.** Land at a city's edge may have layout
  approvals from a village panchayat that lacked authority to grant them — a common
  source of B-khata-type problems.
- **Ancestral and joint family property.** Hindu succession rules can give multiple
  family members interests in a property. Missing a signatory can invalidate a sale.
- **Benami transactions.** The Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act prohibits holding
  property in another's name to disguise the real owner, and has been strengthened.
- **Land acquisition.** The 2013 RFCTLARR Act governs compulsory acquisition and
  compensation.
- **Land litigation volume.** Land and property disputes are very widely cited as
  occupying a large share of India's civil court backlog. **Verify any specific figure**
  — the commonly quoted proportions come from a small number of studies — but the
  qualitative point is not in dispute: **land litigation is endemic, and it is a direct
  consequence of the records problem described above.**

### The digitisation programmes, honestly assessed

Government efforts to fix this are real and substantial:

| Programme | What it does |
|---|---|
| **DILRMP** (Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme) | Umbrella programme to digitise records, computerise registration, and eventually move toward conclusive titling |
| **SVAMITVA** | Drone survey of inhabited rural areas to create property records and title documents for village residents |
| **ULPIN / Bhu-Aadhaar** | A unique 14-character identifier for each land parcel — conceptually the entity-resolution key India's land data has always lacked |
| **NGDRS** | A common national registration software offered to states |
| **State e-registration and e-stamping** | Online stamp duty payment and, increasingly, appointment-based or partly online registration |

**The honest assessment, which matters enormously if you plan to build on this data:**

- Digitisation is **real and improving**, but **uneven between states** and often means
  *scanned images* rather than structured data.
- Older records — frequently the ones that matter for a 30-year title search — are
  commonly **not digitised at all**, or are digitised as poor-quality scans of
  handwritten registers in regional scripts.
- Portals are typically built for **one-off citizen lookup**, not bulk or programmatic
  access: session-based, CAPTCHA-protected, requiring a survey number or document number
  you must already know, and frequently unavailable.
- **Machine-readable APIs are the exception, not the rule.** Assume you will be dealing
  with HTML pages and PDFs unless you verify otherwise for the specific state.
- The programmes do **not** convert presumptive title into conclusive title. That would
  require legislation and a state guarantee, which no state has yet implemented at scale.

**Design consequence:** any India land-data product is fundamentally a
**state-by-state data acquisition and normalisation problem**, and the acquisition half
is harder than the normalisation half. That is a moat if you solve it and a trap if you
underestimate it.

## 3. How the money flows

### Transaction costs on a purchase

| Cost | Typical level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamp duty** | Commonly **4-7% of the higher of consideration or circle rate**, varying by state | The largest single transaction cost. Several states offer a concession (often ~1%) for female buyers |
| **Registration charge** | Commonly ~1% | Paid to the registration department |
| **GST** (under-construction only) | **5% without input tax credit** for non-affordable; **1%** for affordable housing | **No GST on a completed property with an occupancy certificate** — a major driver of buyer preference for ready property |
| **TDS under section 194-IA** | **1% of consideration** where consideration is Rs 50 lakh or more | The *buyer* must deduct and deposit it. A commonly missed compliance step |
| **Brokerage** | Resale: typically 1-2% from each side. Primary: **the developer pays the channel partner 2-6%**; the buyer usually pays nothing | This is why nobody represents the buyer's interest in a primary sale |
| **Legal / title search** | Rs 10,000-50,000+ | Advocate's search and opinion |
| **Loan processing** | ~0.25-1% of loan | Plus legal and technical valuation fees |
| **Society transfer charges** | Varies; capped in some states | Cooperative housing society transfer |
| **Maintenance and sinking fund deposits** | Varies | Often a substantial upfront deposit to the developer |

### Circle rate / ready reckoner / guidance value

Every state publishes a **minimum notified value per unit area** for stamp duty
purposes. It is called different things in different places:

| Name | Where |
|---|---|
| **Circle rate** | Delhi NCR, much of north India |
| **Ready reckoner rate** | Maharashtra |
| **Guidance value** | Karnataka |
| **Market value guidelines** | Tamil Nadu and others |

**Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the actual consideration or this notified
value**, so the notified rate sets a price floor for tax purposes. Where market prices
fall below the notified rate — which happens — buyers pay duty on a value higher than
they paid. Income tax provisions also treat a large gap between consideration and
notified value as taxable income in the hands of buyer and seller.

### The cash component

A factual feature of the market that any data product must account for: historically, a
portion of many resale and land transactions was paid in **unaccounted cash**, with only
part of the price reflected in the registered deed — reducing stamp duty and capital
gains tax. Demonetisation, GST, digitisation of payments, and stricter reporting have
reduced this materially, particularly in primary sales by listed developers, and it
varies enormously by city, segment, and asset type.

**Two implications you should hold onto:**
1. **Registered prices are not always transaction prices.** Any Indian price index built
   from registration data carries this bias, and it varies by market.
2. It is one reason a genuine, trustworthy price-discovery product is both valuable and
   very hard in India — the ground truth is partly undisclosed by design.

### The investor tax layer

- **Capital gains** — long-term treatment applies after a holding period; exemptions
  under **section 54** (reinvest in residential property), **54F** (reinvest proceeds
  from other assets), and **54EC** (specified bonds). The tax rates, indexation, and
  holding periods have been amended repeatedly — **verify the current position**.
- **Home loan deductions** — interest under **section 24(b)** and principal under
  **section 80C** for self-occupied property under the old tax regime. **The new tax
  regime removes most of these deductions**, which has materially changed the tax case
  for buying with a loan. Verify the current rules; this has been in flux.
- **Rental income** — taxed under "income from house property" with a standard deduction
  and interest deduction.

## 4. The transaction spines

### (a) Primary / new booking — the dominant transaction

| # | Stage | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enquiry | — | Generated by portal, Meta/Google ad, channel partner, or hoarding |
| 2 | Tele-calling and qualification | Days | The developer's **pre-sales team** calls (chapter 14) |
| 3 | **Site visit** | — | The critical conversion event. Weekends are peak |
| 4 | Negotiation and booking | Days-weeks | Token/booking amount paid |
| 5 | Allotment letter | — | Developer confirms the specific unit |
| 6 | **RERA verification** | — | Buyer (or their advocate) should check the project's RERA registration, approvals, and QPR filings |
| 7 | **Agreement for Sale** | Weeks | RERA prescribes a model form in most states. **Registered and stamped** — a major cost point |
| 8 | Home loan | Weeks | Bank does its own legal and technical appraisal |
| 9 | **Construction-linked payments** | **Years** | Developer issues **demand letters** at each construction milestone; buyer pays; bank disburses in tranches |
| 10 | Completion, **OC/CC** | — | Occupancy Certificate / Completion Certificate. **Possession without an OC is a serious red flag** |
| 11 | Possession and handover | — | Snagging, fit-out |
| 12 | **Sale deed registration** | — | Final conveyance |
| 13 | Society formation and **conveyance / deemed conveyance** | Years, often delayed | Transfer of the land and common areas to the residents' society. Chronically delayed by developers; several states provide a "deemed conveyance" remedy |

**Note stage 9.** For three to five years, the developer's CRM team issues demand
letters, chases payments, coordinates with banks for tranche disbursement, and handles
buyer queries about construction progress. **This is a long, high-volume, repetitive,
document-and-follow-up-driven relationship** — and it is where the Indian developer's
back office actually spends its time. See chapter 14.

### (b) Resale

Listing (portal and broker networks) → viewings → negotiation → **token/bayana** →
**title due diligence by an advocate** → Agreement for Sale → buyer's home loan →
stamp duty payment (e-stamping) → **registration at the Sub-Registrar Office** →
mutation → society transfer (NOC, transfer fee, share certificate) → handover.

**The advocate's due-diligence checklist** — worth listing, because it is the core
repetitive intellectual work of Indian conveyancing:

- Mother deed and the complete chain of title (typically 30 years)
- **Encumbrance Certificate** for the search period
- Khata / patta / record of rights, and khata extract and certificate
- Latest property tax paid receipts
- Approved building plan and layout approval
- **Occupancy Certificate and Completion Certificate**
- Commencement Certificate
- RERA registration for the project (if applicable)
- Society NOC and share certificate (for apartments)
- Existing loan NOC and release of mortgage, if the property is mortgaged
- Conversion order (if formerly agricultural land)
- Family tree / legal heir certificate where the title passed by inheritance
- Power of attorney documents, where used, and their validity
- Litigation search

### (c) Rental

Broker introduction → viewing → negotiation → agreement → police verification of tenant
(required in many cities) → deposit and rent.

**Two India-specific features:**

- **The 11-month agreement.** Leases of one year or more attract mandatory registration
  and higher stamp duty under the Registration Act. The market's universal workaround is
  an **11-month leave-and-licence or rent agreement**, renewed repeatedly. In
  Maharashtra, registration of leave-and-licence agreements is mandatory regardless.
- **Deposit norms vary dramatically by city.** Bengaluru has historically demanded
  very large deposits (commonly quoted at 6-10 months' rent, though this has softened);
  Mumbai and Delhi are typically 2-3 months. This is a market convention, not a legal
  rule.

The **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** was intended to modernise and standardise landlord-tenant
law, but **tenancy is a state subject and adoption has been limited**. Do not assume it
applies anywhere in particular.

### (d) Home loan

Application → eligibility (income, **FOIR**, **CIBIL** score) → document submission →
**legal opinion** (a panel advocate does an independent title search) → **technical
valuation** (a panel valuer inspects and values) → sanction letter → agreement and
mortgage creation → disbursement (full for ready property, **tranche-wise for
under-construction**).

**LTV is capped by RBI** in bands based on loan size — broadly, higher LTV permitted for
smaller loans, stepping down for larger ones. **Verify the current bands.** Lenders are
banks, **housing finance companies (HFCs)**, and NBFCs. Note that **HDFC Ltd merged into
HDFC Bank in 2023**, consolidating what had been India's largest dedicated housing
financier into a bank.

**Note the duplication in this spine:** the buyer's advocate does a title search, and
then the bank's panel advocate does another one on the same property, and if the buyer
later sells, the next buyer's advocate does a third. **The same 30-year record search is
repeated by different people who cannot rely on each other's work — because there is no
title insurance to make one search authoritative.** That is a structural, recurring,
purely informational duplication, and it is one of the most interesting things in this
chapter.

## 5. Regulation

### RERA — the central regulatory framework

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 requires, in outline:

- **Project registration** with the state RERA authority before marketing or selling
  (above threshold sizes), with disclosure of approvals, land title, layout, timelines,
  and the promoter's track record.
- **Agent registration** — real estate agents must register with the state authority.
- **The 70% escrow rule** — 70% of amounts realised from buyers for a project must be
  kept in a separate account and used only for that project's land and construction
  costs, withdrawable against certification by an engineer, architect and CA. This
  directly targets the diversion of one project's funds to another that caused the
  large insolvencies.
- **Carpet area pricing** — sale must be on carpet area, defined in the Act. This ended
  the practice of quoting on "super built-up" area, which had inflated the apparent size.
- **Quarterly progress reports (QPRs)** — promoters must periodically update project
  status, approvals, and construction progress on the RERA portal.
- **Adjudication** — a complaints and appellate mechanism for buyers.
- **Defect liability** — a statutory period during which the promoter must rectify
  structural and workmanship defects.

**Honest assessment of enforcement:** RERA's design is strong, but **implementation
varies substantially between states**. Some authorities (Maharashtra's MahaRERA is the
most frequently cited as active) publish extensive data and adjudicate large volumes of
complaints; others are less resourced. Some states diluted provisions in their own rules.
**Verify a specific state's rules and practice rather than assuming the central Act's
text is what applies.**

**For a builder, note the QPR obligation.** It is a recurring, structured, deadline-bound
filing that every registered promoter must make for every project, and compiling it
requires pulling construction progress, approvals, and financial data together. That is
a compliance-as-drudgery target of exactly the kind chapter 11 describes.

### The approvals stack

A development typically needs, depending on state and project: land conversion order,
layout approval, building plan sanction, **Commencement Certificate (CC)**, environmental
clearance for larger projects, fire NOC, airport height clearance where relevant,
pollution control board consent, water and sewerage connections, electricity sanction,
lift and other statutory clearances, and finally the **Occupancy Certificate (OC)**.

**Navigating this is the liaison officer's job** (chapter 14) and is the single largest
source of project delay.

### Other regimes

- **FSI / FAR** and building bye-laws — municipal, and the key determinant of what a
  plot is worth. **TDR (Transferable Development Rights)** allows development rights to
  be traded in some cities.
- **Municipal property tax** — assessed and collected by the local body.
- **REITs and SM REITs** — SEBI regulates Real Estate Investment Trusts; listed Indian
  REITs include Embassy Office Parks, Mindspace Business Parks, Brookfield India, and
  Nexus Select Trust (retail). SEBI introduced a framework for **Small and Medium REITs**
  in 2024, lowering the asset threshold and bringing fractional-ownership platforms into
  a regulated structure — a significant and recent change worth tracking.
- **InvITs** — the infrastructure equivalent.
- **FDI** — permitted in construction development subject to conditions; not permitted
  for trading in land or farmhouses.
- **Stamp duty and registration** — state subjects, governed by the Indian Stamp Act as
  adapted by each state, and the Registration Act, 1908.

## 6. Systems of record

### Government systems — and their machine-readability

| System | What it holds | Practical access |
|---|---|---|
| **State registration portals** (IGRS Telangana/AP, Kaveri Karnataka, IGR Maharashtra, Delhi DORIS, TNREGINET) | Registered documents, EC, stamp duty, appointment booking | Web portals for citizen lookup. **Generally session- and CAPTCHA-based, not API-first.** Some offer certified copies online |
| **Land record portals** (Bhulekh, Bhoomi, Dharani, MahaBhulekh, AnyROR Gujarat) | Record of rights, survey details | Same pattern. Query typically requires a survey/khata number you must already have |
| **State RERA portals** (MahaRERA, UP RERA, K-RERA, TS RERA, HARERA etc.) | Project registrations, promoter details, approvals, **QPRs**, complaints and orders | Public search interfaces. **Data is largely PDFs and HTML tables; structured bulk access is rare.** MahaRERA is generally the most data-rich |
| **Municipal property tax portals** | Assessment and tax payment | Per-city |
| **e-Stamping** (SHCIL / StockHolding and state systems) | Stamp duty payment and certificates | Verification interfaces exist |
| **DigiLocker** | Citizen document wallet; some states issue land documents into it | Has APIs; issuer coverage varies |
| **Aadhaar / eKYC** | Identity | **Legally constrained for private entities.** Private companies cannot freely use Aadhaar authentication; permitted routes (offline verification, or authorisation under the amended framework) are narrower than people assume. **Verify before designing any KYC flow around it** |
| **Account Aggregator framework** | Consent-based financial data sharing | Genuinely useful and API-based; relevant to home loan income verification |
| **CERSAI** | Central registry of securitisation and mortgage charges | Records mortgages; a check against undisclosed charges |

**The blunt summary for an engineer:** government real estate data in India is
**publicly viewable but rarely programmatically accessible**, is fragmented by state,
and is often unstructured. Bulk or API access, where it exists at all, is usually a
matter of state-level arrangement rather than a published developer programme.
**Verify per state before designing.**

### Private systems

| Category | Names |
|---|---|
| **Developer CRM and ERP** | Sell.Do, LeadRat, Propacity, Farvision, Highrise, In4Velocity, Zoho CRM, Salesforce, and a great deal of in-house and Excel |
| **Construction / project** | Powerplay, Civilmate, plus Primavera and MS Project on large projects |
| **Portals** | **99acres** (Info Edge), **MagicBricks** (Times Group), **Housing.com / PropTiger / Makaan** (REA India), **NoBroker**, **Square Yards**, CommonFloor |
| **Broker tools** | Largely WhatsApp, Excel, and portal dashboards |
| **Co-living / rental operators** | Stanza Living, Colive, Your-Space; NestAway (distressed exit) |
| **Flex office** | Awfis, WeWork India, Smartworks, Table Space |
| **Society / community management** | **MyGate**, NoBrokerHood, ADDA, ApnaComplex |
| **Home loan marketplaces / DSAs** | BankBazaar, Andromeda, Basic Home Loan, Credila-type specialists |

**MyGate and its peers are worth noting specifically:** gated-community management —
visitor entry, staff attendance, maintenance billing, complaints, notices — reached
genuine scale in India in a way it has not in most markets, because Indian urban housing
is overwhelmingly organised into gated societies with resident associations. That is a
distinctively Indian software category, and the associated administrative work
(maintenance billing, defaulter follow-up, AGM administration, vendor management) is
covered in chapter 14.

## 7. What has been tried in Indian proptech

| Attempt | What it tried | Outcome | The lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NoBroker** | Remove the broker: connect owners and tenants directly for a subscription instead of brokerage | Reached real scale, particularly in rentals in Bengaluru, and expanded into adjacent services (NoBrokerHood, painting, packers, loans) | Disintermediation worked better in **rentals** (high frequency, low value, standardised) than in sales. And note where the money went: **adjacent services, not the core disintermediation** |
| **99acres, MagicBricks, Housing.com** | Classified listing portals monetised by seller/broker subscriptions and developer advertising | The established, durable business model in Indian proptech | **Listings without verification is the persistent quality problem** — duplicate, stale, and fake listings are the universal user complaint |
| **Housing.com** | Ambitious consumer-first portal | High-profile founder turbulence, then acquisition and consolidation into REA India | Execution and capital discipline mattered more than product ambition |
| **Square Yards, PropTiger, Anarock** | Tech-enabled brokerage / channel partner at scale | Operating businesses of real size | **The scaled model in India is tech-enabled distribution for developers**, not consumer disintermediation |
| **NestAway** | Managed rental homes at scale — take on the landlord relationship and manage tenants | Scaled aggressively, then failed and was sold in a distressed transaction | **Managed rental is an operations business with thin margins and high dispute rates.** The Vacasa lesson (chapter 10), in an Indian form |
| **Co-living (Stanza, Colive, Zolo)** | Managed shared living for students and young professionals | Consolidated sharply after rapid growth | Same structure: labour-intensive operations wearing a tech valuation |
| **Fractional ownership platforms** | Retail access to commercial property | **Brought under SEBI's SM REIT framework in 2024** | Regulation arrived and reshaped the category. A live space worth watching |
| **Title insurance in India** | RERA contemplated it; products were notified | Minimal uptake | **The records are too poor to price the risk, and buyers will not pay for an unfamiliar product.** A genuine unsolved problem, not merely an unbuilt one |
| **MyGate / NoBrokerHood** | Digitise gated community operations | Genuine scale | A distinctively Indian category built on a distinctively Indian housing pattern |
| **Rent payment by credit card** (CRED, RedGirraffe and others) | Let tenants pay rent on a card for rewards and float | RBI has tightened the treatment of such flows | Payment-rail arbitrage products are exposed to regulatory reversal |

**The synthesis for an Indian builder:** the durable Indian proptech businesses have been
**distribution for developers** (channel partners at scale, portals, lead generation) and
**operations software for the resident/society layer**. What has repeatedly disappointed
is **managed-operations rollups** (NestAway, co-living) and **consumer disintermediation
of sales**. Meanwhile the genuinely large unsolved problems — title verification, price
discovery, and the delivery risk on under-construction property — remain unsolved
because they are **data and trust problems, not interface problems.**

## 8. Where the human genuinely adds value

- **Physical attendance at the Sub-Registrar Office.** Registration requires the parties
  to appear, with witnesses and biometrics. Not remotely automatable under current law.
- **Title judgment.** Deciding whether a defect in a 30-year chain is fatal, tolerable,
  or curable is legal judgment on incomplete records — the hardest professional call in
  Indian real estate.
- **Local relationships with revenue and municipal officials.** Navigating approvals
  depends on knowing the process, the people, and the practice in a specific office.
- **Litigation risk assessment.** Whether a property is likely to attract a claim from a
  family member, a neighbour, or a government body.
- **The informal trust layer.** Much of Indian real estate runs on reputation within a
  local community — which builder delivers, which broker is honest, which society is
  well-run.
- **Vernacular, in-person negotiation.** Deals are negotiated in Hindi, Marathi, Kannada,
  Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati and more, often in person, often across large cultural and
  educational gaps between parties.
- **Physical inspection of construction quality.**

## 9. Glossary

- **Adangal** — Tamil Nadu cultivation/land record.
- **Agreement for Sale** — The contract to sell, executed before the final sale deed; RERA prescribes a model form.
- **Allotment letter** — Developer's confirmation of a specific unit to a buyer.
- **Bayana / token** — Earnest money paid to hold a deal.
- **Benami** — Property held in another's name to conceal the real owner; prohibited.
- **Bhoomi / Bhulekh / AnyROR / MahaBhulekh** — State online land record portals.
- **Built-up area** — Carpet area plus walls.
- **Carpet area** — Net usable floor area within walls. **RERA mandates pricing on this.**
- **CC (Commencement Certificate)** — Permission to begin construction.
- **CERSAI** — Central registry recording mortgage charges.
- **Channel partner (CP)** — A broker firm selling a developer's inventory for a commission paid by the developer.
- **CIBIL** — The most widely referenced Indian credit bureau/score.
- **Circle rate / ready reckoner rate / guidance value** — State-notified minimum property value for stamp duty.
- **Conveyance / deemed conveyance** — Transfer of land and common areas to the housing society; "deemed" is the statutory remedy when a developer fails to do it.
- **Dharani** — Telangana's integrated land records and registration system.
- **DILRMP** — Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme.
- **EC (Encumbrance Certificate)** — Record of registered transactions on a property for a period.
- **FOIR** — Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio; the Indian equivalent of DTI.
- **FSI / FAR** — Floor Space Index / Floor Area Ratio.
- **Gram panchayat** — Village-level local government; source of some layout approvals.
- **HFC** — Housing Finance Company.
- **Khata (A / B)** — Bengaluru municipal property account; B khata indicates irregularities.
- **Leave and licence** — The Maharashtra rental agreement form; registration mandatory there.
- **Mother deed** — The earliest deed establishing the chain of title.
- **Mutation (dakhil kharij)** — Updating revenue/municipal records after a transfer.
- **NA / DC conversion** — Converting agricultural land to non-agricultural use.
- **OC (Occupancy Certificate)** — Certifies a building is fit and lawful to occupy. **The key document to check.**
- **Patta / Chitta** — Tamil Nadu land title and classification records.
- **QPR** — Quarterly Progress Report filed by a promoter under RERA.
- **RERA** — Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, and the state authorities under it.
- **RTC / Pahani** — Karnataka record of rights, tenancy and crops.
- **Sale deed** — The instrument conveying ownership; registered.
- **Satbara / 7-12 extract** — Maharashtra record of rights.
- **SM REIT** — Small and Medium REIT; SEBI framework introduced in 2024.
- **Society** — Cooperative housing society or apartment owners' association.
- **Super built-up area** — Built-up area plus a share of common areas; the pre-RERA pricing basis.
- **TDR** — Transferable Development Rights.
- **TDS 194-IA** — 1% tax the buyer deducts on property purchases at or above Rs 50 lakh.
- **ULPIN / Bhu-Aadhaar** — Unique 14-character land parcel identifier.

## 10. Verify before you rely on this

**India's real estate rules change frequently, differ by state, and are amended in every
budget. Treat every number below as needing confirmation.**

| Claim | Why it moves | Check against |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamp duty and registration rates** | Per state; changed frequently, sometimes as temporary stimulus | That state's registration department / IGRS |
| **GST rates on under-construction property** | Set by the GST Council; has changed | CBIC / GST Council notifications |
| **Capital gains rules, holding periods, indexation** | **Amended repeatedly in recent budgets** | Income Tax Act as currently amended; a CA |
| **Home loan deductions under old vs new tax regime** | Materially changed; still evolving | Income Tax Department; a CA |
| **RBI LTV caps and risk weights** | Revised periodically | RBI master directions |
| **State RERA rules and enforcement practice** | Each state made its own rules; practice varies widely | That state's RERA authority |
| **Whether a given state portal exposes machine-readable data** | Changes as portals are rebuilt | Test the specific portal directly |
| **Aadhaar eKYC permissibility for private entities** | Legally constrained and repeatedly litigated and amended | UIDAI; counsel. **Do not assume** |
| **SM REIT framework details** | New in 2024; evolving | SEBI |
| **Model Tenancy Act adoption by state** | Limited and slow | State housing departments |
| **Land litigation share of court backlog** | Widely quoted from few studies | The underlying studies; NJDG data |
