# 10 · Niche and Specialty Asset Classes

> Everything outside the residential-sale and office-lease mainstream. Each of these
> sectors has its own workforce, its own software, its own regulator, and its own
> repetitive work — and each is small enough that the big proptech companies mostly
> ignore it. **That is the point of this chapter.** Competition thins out fast as you
> move away from the centre, and several of these sectors have deadline-driven,
> rule-bound processes that are almost untouched.
>
> This is a survey. Each sector gets the essentials: what it is, how the money works,
> the operating spine, the roles, a condensed day or shift, the systems, and a
> repetitive-work table. Depth is weighted toward where the labour is heaviest.

## 1. Short-term rentals (STR)

**What it is.** Furnished properties let by the night through Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com
and direct channels. Operated by individual hosts, by co-hosts managing for owners, or
by professional property management companies.

**Money.** Management companies typically take **15-25% of revenue** (higher than
long-term rental management because the work is far more intense), plus cleaning fees.
Platforms take their own commission. Owners bear furnishing, utilities, and supplies.

**The operating spine.** Listing and photography → pricing (dynamic, adjusted daily) →
inquiry and booking → guest verification → check-in instructions and access → stay →
guest messaging → checkout → **turnover cleaning** → inspection → restock → damage
assessment and claims → review exchange → owner reporting and payout.

**The defining operational problem is the turn.** A same-day checkout and check-in
means a cleaner must complete the property in a few hours, verify supplies, photograph
the result, and flag damage — and if they fail, the next guest arrives to a dirty
property and leaves a review that damages future revenue. Coordinating hundreds of
these across a portfolio, with cleaners who are gig workers, is the core of the business.

**Roles.** Host/owner, co-host, portfolio revenue manager, guest services agent (24/7
messaging), operations coordinator (cleaner scheduling), cleaner, maintenance handyman,
onboarding specialist (bringing new properties into the portfolio), owner-relations
manager.

**Condensed day — STR operations coordinator.** Morning: check today's checkouts and
check-ins against the cleaner schedule; a cleaner has called in sick and three
properties need reassignment before 3pm. Through the day: guest messages arriving
continuously across multiple platforms (Airbnb, Vrbo, direct, WhatsApp), most asking the
same handful of questions — wifi code, check-in time, parking, late checkout; damage
report from a cleaner requires photographs, a claim to the platform, and a conversation
with the guest; a lockbox code fails and a guest is standing outside; pricing review for
the coming month; owner statement queries.

**Systems.** Channel managers and PMS: **Guesty, Hostaway, Hospitable, OwnerRez,
Lodgify**. Dynamic pricing: **PriceLabs, Beyond, Wheelhouse**. Cleaning and turnover:
**Breezeway, Turno (formerly TurnoverBnB)**. Access: smart locks, RemoteLock, Igloohome.
Data: **AirDNA** for market analytics.

**Regulation — the existential risk.** Cities regulate STRs aggressively: registration
requirements, primary-residence rules, night caps, outright bans in some buildings and
districts, and platform-enforcement laws that make the platform delist non-compliant
listings. New York City's Local Law 18 effectively eliminated most short-term rentals in
the city. Barcelona, Amsterdam, and many others have their own regimes. **Any STR
product must treat regulatory compliance as a first-class feature**, and any STR
business must treat regulatory change as a live risk to the asset.

**Prior art.** **Vacasa** is the essential case study: it scaled to be the largest US
vacation rental manager, went public, then declined severely and was acquired. The
lesson is that **STR management is a labour-intensive, low-margin, local operations
business** that does not scale like software. Evolve pursued a lighter-touch model.
Anyone proposing to "roll up STR management with AI" should study Vacasa first.

**Repetitive-work inventory — STR**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guest messaging (repetitive FAQs) | Continuous, hundreds/day at scale | Multi-platform, multi-language, 24/7 | Message → answer |
| Cleaner scheduling and reassignment | Daily | Constraint-solving with unreliable gig labour | Bookings + availability → schedule |
| Turnover verification | Every turn | Photo review against a checklist | Photos → pass/fail |
| Damage assessment and claims | Frequent | Evidence assembly and platform process | Photos + booking → claim |
| Pricing review | Daily/weekly | Tools exist but require oversight | Market data → rates |
| Owner statement production and queries | Monthly | Revenue, fees, expenses per property | Bookings + costs → statement |
| Regulatory registration and renewal | Per property, per jurisdiction | Every city differs | Property → filed registration |

## 2. Hotels and hospitality

**What it is.** The most operationally intense real estate: a business with staff,
inventory, and daily pricing that happens to occupy a building.

**The three-way split** that confuses newcomers: the **owner** holds the asset, a
**management company** operates it under a Hotel Management Agreement, and a **brand**
(Marriott, Hilton, IHG) licenses the flag and supplies the reservation system and loyalty
programme. Three entities, three fee streams, sometimes three different agendas.

**Money.** Owner takes the residual. Manager takes a base fee (percentage of revenue)
plus an incentive fee (percentage of profit). Brand takes a franchise/royalty fee plus
marketing and loyalty contributions. Online travel agencies (OTAs) take **15-25%** of
bookings made through them — which is why direct booking is a strategic obsession.

**Key metrics:** ADR (average daily rate), occupancy, **RevPAR** (revenue per available
room = ADR x occupancy), and GOPPAR (gross operating profit per available room).

**Roles.** General manager, director of operations, **revenue manager** (sets prices
daily), director of sales and marketing, sales manager (group and corporate business),
catering and events manager, front office manager, front desk agent, **night auditor**,
concierge, reservations agent, executive housekeeper, **room attendant**, houseman,
laundry, chief engineer, maintenance tech, F&B manager, chefs and servers, controller,
accounts payable clerk, HR manager.

**Condensed shift — night auditor.** Works 11pm-7am. Handles overnight front desk. At
a set time runs the **night audit**: closes the business day in the property management
system, posts room and tax charges to every occupied room, reconciles all cash and card
transactions from every outlet (front desk, restaurant, bar, spa), resolves
discrepancies, produces the daily revenue report for management, and rolls the system
date forward. **It is a nightly, procedural, reconciliation-heavy closing process** — and
it exists in this form largely because a 24-hour business needs a moment to draw a line
under the day. It is a striking example of a purely procedural job in real estate.

**Condensed day — revenue manager.** Reviews yesterday's pickup (bookings taken),
compares on-the-books position against the same time last year, checks the competitive
set's rates (**STR benchmarking reports** — confusingly the same acronym as short-term
rental), adjusts rates and restrictions across dozens of rate plans and channels,
manages group blocks, forecasts. Increasingly assisted by revenue management systems
(IDeaS, Duetto) but the overrides and judgment remain human.

**Systems.** PMS: **Oracle Opera**, Mews, Cloudbeds, Agilysys. Revenue management:
IDeaS, Duetto. Channel management and distribution: SynXis, Derbysoft. Benchmarking:
CoStar's STR reports. POS: Micros, Toast.

**Repetitive-work inventory — hotels**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night audit reconciliation | Nightly | Procedural close across multiple systems | Day's transactions → closed books + reports |
| Rate loading across channels | Daily | Many rate plans across many channels | Pricing decision → distributed rates |
| Group booking administration | Continuous | Contracts, rooming lists, billing instructions | Contract → blocks and bills |
| Housekeeping assignment and inspection | Daily | Room status changes constantly | Occupancy → assignments |
| Guest request handling | Continuous | Repetitive requests via phone, app, messaging | Request → fulfilment |
| Invoice and city-ledger reconciliation | Daily/monthly | Corporate billing disputes | Charges → collected revenue |
| Compliance: fire, food safety, licensing | Periodic | Multi-agency inspections | Inspection → certificate |

## 3. Senior housing and care

**What it is.** A spectrum from **independent living** (housing with services, minimal
care), through **assisted living** (help with daily activities), **memory care**
(secured dementia care), to **skilled nursing** (clinical, medically regulated). The
further along that spectrum, the more it is a healthcare business and the less it is
real estate.

**Money.** Independent and assisted living are largely private-pay monthly fees plus
care level charges. Skilled nursing depends heavily on **Medicaid and Medicare
reimbursement**, which brings intense clinical documentation and billing requirements.
Owner/operator splits are common, often via a structure that separates the property
from the operating business for tax and regulatory reasons.

**The defining operating metrics:** **census** (occupancy), care level mix, and
**labour cost** — particularly agency staffing, which is expensive and has been a
persistent post-2020 pressure.

**Roles.** Executive director, sales/move-in counsellor, community relations director,
**resident care director**, nurses (RN/LPN), certified nursing assistants, med techs,
**MDS coordinator** (in skilled nursing — assembles the clinical assessment that drives
reimbursement), activities director, dietary manager, business office manager,
maintenance director, housekeeping, **compliance/survey coordinator**, regional
operations, transitions coordinator.

**The compliance burden is extreme and is the story of this sector.** State licensing
surveys, federal certification for Medicare/Medicaid participation, incident reporting,
care plan documentation, medication administration records, staffing ratio evidence,
resident rights documentation, infection control protocols. **A very large share of
clinical staff time goes to documentation rather than care** — a complaint universal
across the sector and one of the clearest quality-of-life-driven automation arguments
anywhere in this guide.

**Condensed day — move-in sales counsellor.** Fields inquiries (families in distress,
often at a crisis moment), conducts tours, follows up on a long pipeline (the decision
cycle can be months), assembles move-in paperwork — which is extensive: assessments,
financial documentation, physician forms, medication lists, advance directives — and
coordinates the assessment that determines care level and pricing.

**Systems.** **PointClickCare** (dominant in skilled nursing), MatrixCare, Yardi Senior
Living, Eldermark. CRM: **Enquire**, Sherpa. Market data: NIC MAP.

**Repetitive-work inventory — senior housing**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical documentation and care plans | Continuous | Regulated content, per resident | Observation → record |
| MDS assessment assembly | Per resident, periodic | Drives reimbursement; accuracy is financial | Clinical data → assessment |
| Survey/inspection preparation | Periodic | Evidence assembly across many domains | Records → survey binder |
| Move-in packet assembly | Per move-in | Many forms from many parties | Family + physician docs → complete file |
| Staffing schedule and agency fill | Daily | Constraint-solving under shortage | Shifts + availability → roster |
| Billing and reimbursement | Monthly | Payer rules differ | Care delivered → claim |
| Family communication | Continuous | Emotionally sensitive, repetitive questions | Question → update |

## 4. Student housing

**What it is.** Purpose-built accommodation near universities, leased **by the bed**
rather than by the unit, usually on 12-month leases aligned to the academic year, with
parental guarantors.

**The defining feature is the turn.** Nearly **100% of the property turns over within
about a week** in late summer. Hundreds of beds must be cleaned, repaired, inspected,
and re-let simultaneously, with move-outs and move-ins overlapping. It is a logistics
extreme with no equivalent in conventional multifamily.

**The second defining feature is the annual pre-lease cycle.** Leasing for next August
begins the previous September. The entire year's revenue is determined by a pre-lease
velocity race tracked weekly against last year's pace.

**Roles.** General manager, leasing manager, **community assistants** (student staff who
do much of the leasing and events work), maintenance, turn coordinator, resident
services, roommate matching coordinator, university liaison, regional manager.

**Specific repetitive work:** roommate matching (preference-based allocation across
hundreds of applicants), guarantor verification and chasing, individual liability leases
(each bed is its own lease, so a four-bed unit has four leases, four deposits, four
delinquency tracks), and the turn's inspection-and-charge process across hundreds of
beds in days.

**Systems.** Entrata and RealPage student modules, Yardi RentCafe Student.

## 5. Self-storage

**What it is.** Rented storage units. Notable for having the **lowest staffing per
dollar of asset value** of any real estate sector — many facilities are unmanned or
staffed part-time, with kiosks, remote management, and call centres.

**Money.** Month-to-month rentals. The business model is built on two things:
**ECRI (Existing Customer Rate Increases)** — customers are acquired at a low rate and
increased regularly once their belongings are inside and moving is a hassle — and
**tenant insurance/protection plans**, a high-margin attachment.

**The interesting process is the lien sale.** When a tenant stops paying, the operator
follows a **statutory process** — specific notice periods, specific content, publication
requirements in some states, then an auction of the unit's contents. It is entirely
prescribed by state law, entirely deadline-driven, and it happens continuously at scale.
**A rule-bound, per-state, document-generating, deadline-critical process running at
volume with minimal staff is an unusually clean automation target.**

**Roles.** Store manager, district manager, call centre agent, revenue manager,
auction coordinator, maintenance.

**Systems.** **Storable** (storEDGE, SiteLink), Easy Storage Solutions, Yardi
Self Storage. Auctions: StorageTreasures.

## 6. Industrial, logistics, and data centres

### Industrial and logistics

Warehouses, distribution centres, cold storage, last-mile facilities, flex/light
industrial. The strongest-performing sector of the 2020s, driven by e-commerce and
supply chain restructuring. Leases are 3-10 years, often triple-net, with relatively
simple terms compared to office or retail — **which makes it the easiest asset class
for lease data work.** Key value drivers: clear height, dock doors, trailer parking,
power, and proximity to population.

Roles resemble commercial real estate generally (chapter 06), with specialists in
industrial brokerage, site selection, and build-to-suit development.

### Data centres

Currently the most capital-hungry sector in real estate, driven by AI compute demand.
Worth understanding as a distinct discipline.

**The product tiers:** **powered shell** (a building with power delivered, fitted out by
the tenant), **colocation** (the operator provides racks, power, and cooling to many
customers), **hyperscale** (purpose-built for a single large cloud tenant),
**build-to-suit**.

**The binding constraint is power, not land.** Site selection is driven by power
availability, the utility's **interconnection queue** (which can run years), fibre
routes, water for cooling, and increasingly local political acceptance. Pricing is
denominated in **dollars per kilowatt per month**, not per square foot — an important
conceptual shift for anyone coming from conventional real estate.

**Roles.** Site selection and land acquisition specialists, power procurement and energy
markets specialists, development managers, commissioning agents (the multi-level testing
process before a facility goes live is highly structured and documentation-heavy),
**critical facility technicians** working 24/7 shift rotations, data centre operations
managers, capacity planners, remote hands technicians, security officers, and specialist
brokers.

**Repetitive work worth noting:** commissioning documentation (Levels 1-5 testing, each
with scripts and evidence), capacity and power tracking, change management for anything
touching live infrastructure, incident and maintenance logging under strict uptime
commitments, customer capacity reporting, and interconnection application tracking
across utilities.

**Systems.** DCIM (data centre infrastructure management): Nlyte, Sunbird, EkkoSense.
Plus conventional BAS/BMS at high redundancy.

## 7. Manufactured housing and mobile home parks

**What it is.** Communities where residents typically **own their home but rent the
land** it sits on. The operator collects **lot rent** and often controls utilities.

**Why institutional capital moved in:** extremely low turnover (moving a manufactured
home is expensive and often impractical), low capital expenditure for the operator, and
resilient demand as the most affordable unsubsidised housing in the US.

**That same dynamic makes the sector politically contentious** — residents cannot easily
leave, so lot rent increases attract regulatory attention and there is a growing
resident-owned community movement supporting resident purchases.

**Roles.** Community manager (often living on site), regional manager, maintenance,
collections, utility billing administrator, home sales specialist.

**Repetitive work:** utility submetering and billing, lot rent collection and
delinquency, home title transfers (manufactured homes are often titled like vehicles,
which is its own paperwork regime), and compliance with state manufactured-housing
statutes distinct from ordinary landlord-tenant law.

**Systems.** Rent Manager, ManageAmerica, Yardi.

## 8. Land, agricultural, and timber

**Raw land brokerage.** Selling undeveloped parcels — a very different sale from
housing, driven by entitlement potential, utility availability, topography, soil,
access, and zoning. Buyers are developers and investors.

**Agricultural.** Farmland is valued on productive capacity and commodity economics.
Ownership is often multi-generational and fractionalised through inheritance — a
significant administrative problem in itself. Income structures: **cash rent** (fixed
per acre) or **crop share** (a percentage of the harvest). A farm manager role exists
to run land on behalf of absentee or institutional owners, and generates substantial
recurring paperwork: leases, crop reports, input purchases, government programme
enrolment, and landowner reporting.

**Mineral, water, and timber rights.** Rights can be severed from the surface and owned
separately — a source of considerable title complexity, and of a specialised industry in
**mineral rights research and title work**, much of which is deeply manual record
searching. Water rights in the western US are their own legal universe. Timber is
managed on multi-decade harvest cycles.

**Conservation easements and hunting leases** are additional income structures.

**Systems.** AcreValue, Land.com/LandWatch, AgriWebb and farm management platforms,
county GIS.

## 9. Distressed: foreclosure, auction, REO, tax liens

**The foreclosure timeline** varies fundamentally by state:
- **Judicial foreclosure** states require a court proceeding — slow, often a year or more.
- **Non-judicial** states allow a trustee sale under a power-of-sale clause — much faster.

Either way the process is a sequence of **statutorily prescribed notices with strict
timing and content requirements**, followed by a sale. Errors invalidate the process.
This is, once again, deadline-and-document work with severe consequences for error.

**After the sale**, unsold properties become **REO** (Real Estate Owned) on the lender's
books, managed by **REO asset managers** who order **BPOs** (chapter 05), engage
**property preservation** vendors, and list the property for sale.

**Property preservation** deserves attention because it is a large, invisible, heavily
manual gig-labour industry. Field vendors visit vacant properties to secure them, change
locks, winterise plumbing, cut grass, remove debris, and document everything with
**photographs against a prescribed checklist**. They are paid against an **allowable**
schedule set by the investor (Fannie, Freddie, FHA, or a private servicer), must submit
photo evidence for every line item, and face **chargebacks** when documentation is
inadequate. The photo documentation and invoice-substantiation burden is the industry's
defining pain.

**Roles.** Default servicing specialist, foreclosure specialist, trustee staff, REO
asset manager, BPO agent, preservation vendor coordinator, field inspector, auction
platform staff, title curative specialist for distressed files, tax lien investor,
note buyer.

**Tax liens and deeds.** Counties sell delinquent property tax obligations. In **tax
lien** states investors buy the lien and earn statutory interest, with a possible path
to foreclosure; in **tax deed** states the property itself is sold. Both are governed by
per-county rules, per-county auction platforms, and hard redemption deadlines — a
research-and-calendar-heavy business.

**Systems.** Auction.com, Hubzu, Xome, ServiceLink; Aspen Grove and Pyramid Platform for
field services; county tax sale portals.

## 10. 1031 exchanges, DSTs, and opportunity zones

**1031 exchange** lets a US investor defer capital gains tax by exchanging one investment
property for another. The rules are unforgiving:

- Sale proceeds must be held by a **Qualified Intermediary** — the seller may not touch
  them.
- Replacement property must be **identified within 45 days** of the sale.
- The exchange must **close within 180 days**.
- No extensions for ordinary hardship.

**A pure, high-stakes, statutory calendar problem.** The QI industry exists to
administer it and is itself lightly regulated, which has produced failures where
intermediaries misused funds.

**DSTs (Delaware Statutory Trusts)** package fractional interests in institutional
property that qualify as replacement property for a 1031 — solving the problem of an
investor who must deploy proceeds within 45 days.

**Opportunity Zones** offer capital gains deferral and exclusion for investments in
designated areas, with their own compliance and reporting requirements over a long
holding period.

## 11. Wholesaling and fix-and-flip

**What it is.** The "we buy houses" market: finding distressed or motivated sellers,
contracting to buy at a discount, then either **assigning the contract** to an investor
for a fee (wholesaling) or buying, renovating and reselling (flipping).

**Why it matters here.** It is large, tech-hungry, lightly capitalised, and full of
software buyers — and it is also the corner of real estate with the **highest
concentration of legal risk for anyone building outbound-contact tooling**.

**The workflow.** Build a list (tax delinquency, probate, code violations, absentee
owners, high equity, pre-foreclosure) → **skip trace** to find phone numbers → contact
at volume by cold call, SMS, ringless voicemail, and direct mail → qualify motivation →
make an offer → contract → assign or close → renovate → resell.

**The legal problem.** Cold outbound contact at volume to consumers is exactly what the
**TCPA** and Do-Not-Call rules restrict, and this sector has generated substantial class
action litigation. Statutory damages of $500-1,500 per call or text, applied to campaigns
sending tens of thousands of messages, are business-ending. Add the FCC's position that
**AI-generated voices are artificial voices** requiring prior express consent, and
"AI cold-calling for real estate leads" is close to a worked example of what not to build.

Separately, several states have **restricted or licensed wholesaling itself**, on the
theory that marketing a property you do not own is brokerage requiring a licence.

**Systems.** PropStream, BatchLeads, DealMachine (driving for dollars), REsimpli,
Investorlift, Carrot (websites), Launch Control and similar SMS platforms.

**The honest verdict for a builder:** there is real demand and real willingness to pay
here, but the obvious products are legally exposed. The defensible opportunities are on
the **analysis and operations** side — deal analysis, renovation scoping and budgeting,
contractor management, disposition to buyer lists — not the outbound contact side.

## 12. Co-living, coworking, and flex space

**Coworking and flex.** Post-WeWork, the model persists in a more disciplined form:
management agreements and revenue shares with landlords rather than long leases arbitraged
against short memberships. Operators: IWG/Regus, Industrious, Awfis and WeWork India in
India. The operational work is membership administration, space allocation, meeting room
booking, community events, and billing.

**Co-living.** Shared apartments with private bedrooms and managed common space, rented
by the room with flexible terms. The economics are labour-heavy: more residents per
square foot means more turnover, more disputes, and more cleaning. In India the sector
grew rapidly and then consolidated sharply — **NestAway is the cautionary case**,
having scaled and then been sold in a distressed transaction. Stanza Living, Colive and
others continue in the student/young-professional segment. See chapter 13.

**The pattern across both:** these are **operating businesses wearing real estate
clothing**, with thin margins and high staff intensity. Software helps; it does not
change the arithmetic.

## 13. Cross-sector synthesis

Reading these twelve sectors together, four patterns matter for finding use cases.

**1. Statutory processes running at volume with minimal staff.** Self-storage lien
sales, foreclosure notices, 1031 deadlines, STR registration, manufactured-home title
transfers. Each is a per-jurisdiction, rule-bound, deadline-critical, document-generating
process. Small teams run them at volume, errors are expensive, and almost nobody has
built good tooling. **This is the strongest common pattern in the chapter.**

**2. Documentation burdens that crowd out the actual job.** Senior housing clinical
documentation, property preservation photo evidence, data centre commissioning records,
hotel night audit. In each, a skilled or caring worker spends a large fraction of their
time producing evidence rather than doing the work.

**3. Coordination of unreliable distributed labour.** STR cleaners, preservation field
vendors, senior housing agency staff. Scheduling, verification, and chasing across a gig
or contract workforce.

**4. Sectors where the incumbent solution is "hire cheaply and hope".** Most of these
sectors are too small to attract serious software investment, so the operating answer is
a person with a spreadsheet. That is an opportunity — but remember it also means small
markets and buyers with modest budgets. **Niche means less competition and less money.
Size the market honestly before committing.**

## 14. Verify before you rely on this

| Claim | Why it moves | Check against |
|---|---|---|
| STR regulation in any specific city | Changes constantly and materially | The city's own ordinance |
| Vacasa, NestAway and other company outcomes | Transactions and restructurings | Company filings; contemporaneous reporting |
| Management fee ranges in every sector | Market-negotiated | Actual agreements; sector associations |
| Self-storage lien sale procedures | Per state statute | That state's self-service storage facility act |
| Foreclosure timelines and judicial/non-judicial status | Per state; occasionally amended | State statute; counsel |
| 1031 rules and any legislative proposals to limit them | 45/180-day rules stable; political status varies | IRS; current legislation |
| Wholesaling licensing restrictions | Several states have acted recently | That state's real estate commission |
| Data centre power and interconnection dynamics | Moving very fast | Utility filings; sector research |
| Senior housing reimbursement and survey rules | Federal and state, frequently updated | CMS; state licensing agency |
| Sector employment figures | Not reliably published for most niches | BLS where available; sector associations |

**Method note.** This chapter was written from domain knowledge after a rate limit ended
the research fan-out, and it covers twelve sectors at survey depth rather than one in
detail. Structural descriptions are standard practice; specific fee ranges and
procedures are indicative. Every statutory process named here is jurisdictional —
treat the descriptions as *shape*, never as the rule for a particular place.
