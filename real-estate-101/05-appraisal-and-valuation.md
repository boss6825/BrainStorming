# 05 · Appraisal, Valuation, and Property Assessment

> Every property that changes hands, gets taxed, gets insured, or backs a loan eventually needs
> one number attached to it: what is this specific property worth, right now, to a typical
> buyer? Appraisal and valuation is the machinery that produces that number and, just as
> important, tries to keep the person producing it independent of everyone who wants a
> particular answer. A lender will not fund a mortgage against collateral an independent
> opinion says is worth less than the loan; a government cannot tax fairly without a
> defensible estimate of what every parcel in the county is worth; an insurer, a court, and
> a divorcing couple all eventually need the same kind of number for different reasons. For
> most of the 20th century "the machinery" meant a licensed human physically visiting the
> property. Today it increasingly means a statistical model that never visits at all — and
> the single biggest fact to hold onto in this chapter is that the model has already won a
> large and growing share of US mortgage lending (where a lender just needs the number
> confirmed fast and cheap) while making almost no progress in property tax assessment,
> commercial investment underwriting, or litigation (where someone needs to be able to stand
> behind the number, under oath if necessary). That split is not an accident of technology —
> it is a direct consequence of who is legally allowed to sign their name to the answer.
