# 14 · Who Actually Works in Indian Real Estate — Roles, Days, and Workflows

> Chapter 13 covered how the Indian market and transaction are structured. This chapter
> is the people half: who does the work, what their day actually looks like, what tools
> they use, what they are measured on, and **what they cost per month** — because in
> India the cost of the human alternative is the number that decides whether an
> automation idea is a business.
>
> Two things in this chapter deserve your attention above the rest. **Section 5** covers
> the WhatsApp layer — the fact that most Indian real estate runs on group chats, voice
> notes and forwarded PDFs rather than on any system of record. **Section 4.9** covers
> the India-based analysts who perform US and UK real estate back-office work overnight:
> simultaneously your closest competitor and your most accessible customer.

## 1. The employment landscape

Indian real estate employment splits into five worlds that barely overlap:

| World | Who | Formality |
|---|---|---|
| **Listed and large developers** | DLF, Godrej, Lodha, Prestige, Brigade, Sobha and similar | Professional HR, structured roles, CRM and ERP systems |
| **Regional and local builders** | Thousands of small developers | Informal, family-run, Excel and WhatsApp |
| **The broker market** | From national channel-partner firms down to the neighbourhood property dealer | **Overwhelmingly informal.** RERA requires agent registration; compliance is partial |
| **Construction labour** | Site workers, largely migrant, engaged through **thekedars** (labour contractors) | Largely informal; the largest headcount by far |
| **Services** | Facility management, security, housekeeping | Organised at scale; large employers |

**Construction is generally described as India's second-largest employer after
agriculture.** Even allowing for definitional variation, the qualitative point stands:
this is a sector defined by an enormous, largely informal workforce, with a comparatively
thin professional layer on top.

**The informality is a design constraint, not a footnote.** If your product assumes the
counterparty has an email address they check, a company domain, a CRM, or a registration
number, a large share of the market is outside your addressable universe. Products that
work over **phone and WhatsApp**, in **Indian languages**, with **no onboarding**, reach
a market that products requiring accounts and logins do not.

tedious work with a distinctive property: **the "buyer" has no budget authority alone but
also no vendor lock-in**, and the pain is felt directly by the person who would adopt the
tool.

## 3. What the money looks like

The lead economics of Indian developer sales, because they explain the entire pre-sales
machine. These are **indicative industry benchmarks that vary enormously** by city,
price band, and project — verify against real campaign data before relying on them.

```
Digital ad spend
      |
      v
   LEAD  (cost per lead commonly quoted in the hundreds to low thousands of rupees)
      |   many leads are junk: wrong numbers, budget mismatch, form-fill bots
      v
   CONNECT   (only a fraction of leads ever answer the phone)
      |
      v
   SITE VISIT   (a small percentage of leads; the pre-sales team's actual KPI)
      |
      v
   BOOKING   (a minority of site visits)
```

**The whole pre-sales tele-calling industry exists because of the attrition in that
funnel.** Each stage loses most of what entered it, so developers employ large calling
teams to work volume. That is precisely the shape of work that looks automatable — and
precisely where India's consumer-contact regulations, language diversity, and low labour
cost complicate the case. Hold both thoughts.

## 4. Days in the life

### 4.1 Pre-sales tele-caller — day in the life

**The single highest-headcount, highest-repetition role in Indian real estate sales, and
the most obvious agent target in this chapter.**

- **Who they are:** typically 20-26, graduate or undergraduate, often in their first or
  second job. High attrition. Works from a developer's or agency's office in a bay of
  20-60 callers.
- **Measured on:** dials per day, connects, and above all **site visits scheduled and
  actually attended**.
- **Their stack:** a CRM (Sell.Do, LeadRat, Zoho, Salesforce, or an in-house tool), a
  dialler or just a phone, **WhatsApp Web** for sending brochures and location pins, an
  Excel sheet the CRM cannot replace, and a printed or on-screen script.

**A typical day**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 9:30 | Login. Overnight leads from Meta, Google, 99acres, MagicBricks and Housing are in the queue. Fresh leads are called first — **speed matters enormously** |
| 9:45 | Dialling begins. Most calls go unanswered. Of those answered, many are "just browsing", wrong numbers, or people who never filled a form |
| 11:00 | A qualified conversation: budget, configuration (2BHK/3BHK), location preference, purpose (self-use or investment), loan requirement, timeline. Logged in the CRM |
| 11:15 | Send project brochure, price sheet, and location pin **on WhatsApp**. Most information transfer happens here, not by email |
| 12:30 | Follow-up calls to yesterday's and last week's leads. **The same conversation, repeated** |
| 14:00 | Confirm this weekend's site visits. Re-confirm the ones booked earlier — **no-shows are the biggest source of wasted effort**, so each visit is confirmed two or three times |
| 15:30 | Cold-calling a purchased or scraped database. Low yield, high volume |
| 17:00 | Update the CRM: disposition every call, set follow-up dates. **Often done in a rush at the end of the day, so data quality suffers** |
| 18:30 | Daily report to the team lead: dials, connects, visits booked |
| 19:30 | Late calls — many working prospects only answer in the evening |

- **Volume:** **commonly 80-150 dials a day**, with a much smaller number of meaningful
  conversations. Verify against real floor data; it varies by lead quality.
- **What they complain about** (consistent across job-review sites and forums): unrealistic
  targets, poor lead quality, pressure, low pay, long hours including weekends, and
  monotony. Attrition is high, which means **constant retraining is a real cost to the
  employer** — an argument for automation that has nothing to do with per-call cost.

**Repetitive-work inventory — pre-sales tele-caller**

| Task | Frequency | Minutes each | Why it's still manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dialling and re-dialling unanswered leads | 80-150/day | 0.5-2 | Most calls do not connect | Lead list → connect or retry |
| The qualification conversation | 10-30/day | 3-8 | Same questions every time, in several languages | Conversation → structured lead record |
| Sending collateral on WhatsApp | 20-50/day | 1-3 | Manual file selection and sending | Lead + project → brochure sent |
| **Site visit confirmation and re-confirmation** | 10-40/day | 1-3 | No-show prevention requires repeated contact | Booking → confirmed visit |
| CRM disposition and follow-up scheduling | Every call | 0.5-2 | Manual data entry after each call | Call outcome → CRM record |
| Daily reporting | Daily | 15-30 | Assembled by hand | Call data → report |

**A necessary caution before you build here.** This looks like the perfect voice-agent
use case, and it partly is — but note three things. First, the conversations happen in
**Hindi, Marathi, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, Bengali, Gujarati and code-mixed English**,
often on poor mobile connections. Second, **the caller costs Rs 15,000-30,000 a month**,
which sets a low ceiling on what you can charge per seat replaced. Third, India's
telecom regulator (TRAI) regulates commercial communications and unsolicited commercial
calls through a registration and consent framework — **verify the current rules before
designing an outbound calling product.** The strongest version of this product is
probably not "replace the caller" but "make each caller handle three times the volume
with better data capture".

### 4.2 Site sales manager on a Sunday — day in the life

- **Who they are:** the person at the project site or experience centre who converts a
  visit into a booking. Paid a modest base plus meaningful **booking incentives**.
- **Their stack:** CRM on a tablet or phone, the price sheet and payment plan (an Excel
  the sales head updates), the sample flat, WhatsApp.

**Sunday is the working week.** Site visits concentrate at weekends, so the sales team's
rhythm is inverted — Tuesday or Wednesday is the day off, and Saturday and Sunday are
the revenue days.

**A typical Sunday:** arrive before the first appointment; the day is a sequence of
walk-ins and scheduled visits, each running 45-90 minutes — welcome, project pitch,
sample flat walkthrough, unit selection on the layout, price and payment plan
discussion, negotiation on discounts and freebies, and the push for a **token amount
today**. Between visits: WhatsApp follow-ups with last week's visitors, calls to
prospects who did not show, coordination with the pre-sales team, and updating the CRM.
Peak weekends can mean a dozen or more visits in a day with overlapping families waiting.

**Repetitive-work inventory — site sales**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| The project pitch | Every visit | Delivered in person, in the visitor's language | — |
| Price and payment plan calculation | Every serious visit | Excel with discounts, GST, stamp duty, plan variants | Unit + terms → total cost sheet |
| **Cost sheet generation** | Every serious visit | Manually assembled and often WhatsApped as a photo of a screen | Unit selection → itemised cost |
| Post-visit follow-up | Continuous | WhatsApp and calls | Visit → next step |
| CRM updating | Every visit | Rushed between visits | Visit → record |
| Inventory availability checks | Continuous | Which units are held, blocked or sold changes hourly on a launch weekend | Query → live availability |

### 4.3 CRM / post-sales executive — day in the life

**The most under-appreciated repetitive role in Indian real estate.** Note that in India
"CRM" in a developer means the **post-sales** function, not the software.

- **Who they are:** manages a portfolio of booked customers from booking through to
  possession — a relationship lasting **three to five years**.
- **Measured on:** **collection efficiency** (how much of what was demanded actually
  came in), documentation turnaround, and customer complaints.
- **Their stack:** the developer's CRM/ERP (Farvision, Highrise, In4Velocity, Sell.Do,
  or in-house), Excel, email, and **WhatsApp for everything that actually needs a
  response**.

**A typical day**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 10:00 | Run the **demand list**: which customers have hit a construction milestone that triggers a payment |
| 10:30 | **Generate and issue demand letters** — the amount due, the milestone reached, the due date, GST and other charges. Often generated from the ERP, then checked and corrected by hand |
| 11:30 | **Collections follow-up.** Call and WhatsApp customers whose payments are overdue. Some are waiting on their bank's tranche disbursement; some are unhappy about construction delay and withholding payment deliberately |
| 13:00 | **Bank coordination**: chase the customer's bank for the disbursement, send the bank the demand letter and construction progress evidence, follow up on the sanctioned tranche |
| 14:30 | Documentation: agreement execution, stamp duty and registration coordination, name transfers, NOCs for resale before possession, loan-related NOCs |
| 16:00 | Customer queries — the recurring set: "what is the construction status", "when is possession", "why has the demand come now", "give me a payment receipt", "give me the tax certificate for my loan" |
| 17:30 | Update collection tracker and MIS; report the day's collections |
| 18:30 | Possession-stage work for a completing tower: final demand, dues clearance, handover appointment scheduling, snag list coordination |

**Repetitive-work inventory — CRM / post-sales**

| Task | Frequency | Minutes each | Why it's still manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand letter generation and issue** | Dozens-hundreds/month | 5-15 | ERP output needs checking; delivery by email and WhatsApp | Milestone + unit → demand letter |
| **Collections follow-up** | Daily, dozens | 3-10 | Requires persistent human contact, in language | Overdue list → payment or commitment |
| **Bank disbursement coordination** | Continuous | 10-30 | Three-way chase between customer, bank and developer | Demand → disbursed tranche |
| Payment receipt and ledger queries | Daily | 3-10 | Customers cannot self-serve | Query → statement |
| Construction status updates | Daily, repetitive | 3-10 | Same question from many customers | Query → status |
| Documentation coordination | Continuous | Varies | Registration appointments, stamp duty, signatures | Request → executed document |
| Possession handover coordination | Per unit | Hours | Dues clearance, snagging, appointment | Unit → handover |
| Collection MIS reporting | Daily/weekly | 30-90 | Assembled in Excel | Transactions → MIS |

**Why this role matters for your purposes:** it combines four of the eight repetitive-work
shapes from chapter 00 — document generation, chasing, deadline tracking, and
reconciliation — in one job, at high volume, with a clear financial KPI (collections)
that a buyer can measure. That combination is rare.

### 4.4 Independent neighbourhood broker — day in the life

- **Who they are:** the local property dealer, often with a small office near the market
  or operating entirely from a phone and a two-wheeler. May or may not be RERA-registered.
- **Their stack:** **WhatsApp above all**, a phone, sometimes a portal subscription
  (99acres/MagicBricks), a diary or Excel of listings, and local relationships with
  society secretaries, watchmen, and other brokers.

**A typical day:** morning starts in WhatsApp — a dozen broker groups where requirements
and inventory are posted continuously ("Need 3BHK rent Koramangala budget 60k",
"Available 2BHK sale HSR 1.35 Cr semi-furnished"). Matching a requirement against
remembered or forwarded inventory is the core skill. Then calls to owners to check
whether a property is still available and at what price — **inventory goes stale
constantly and there is no system that knows**. Afternoon: property showings, riding
between properties with clients. Evening: negotiation calls, coordination with the other
side's broker on splitting brokerage, follow-ups. Paperwork — agreements, token receipts
— is handled through a known deed writer or a lawyer.

**Repetitive-work inventory — independent broker**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading and matching WhatsApp group traffic** | Continuous, hundreds of messages/day | Unstructured text and images across many groups | Messages → matched requirement/inventory |
| **Inventory freshness verification** | Continuous | Requires calling the owner; nothing tracks status | Listing → still available or not |
| Requirement capture | Per client | Verbal, in language, no CRM | Conversation → requirement |
| Showing scheduling and coordination | Daily | Owner, tenant and broker calendars by phone | Availability → visit |
| Follow-up with clients and other brokers | Continuous | Relationship-based | — |
| Documentation coordination | Per deal | Handed to a deed writer or lawyer | Deal terms → agreement |

**Note the first two rows.** An enormous amount of Indian brokerage value is simply
**knowing what is currently available and genuinely for sale at a real price** — and
that knowledge exists only in brokers' heads and WhatsApp groups because there is no
MLS. That is the market's central information problem (chapter 13), visible at the level
of one person's day.

### 4.5 Channel partner relationship manager — day in the life

Employed by a developer to keep broker firms selling their inventory. The day is:
visiting or calling CP offices; conducting product training so CP staff can pitch the
project accurately; distributing updated inventory, price lists and offers (by WhatsApp,
inevitably); **registering and tagging leads** so the right CP gets credit for a booking
— a constant source of dispute; chasing CPs to bring site visits; and **processing
brokerage invoices and payouts**, which is where the relationship is actually tested.

**Repetitive work:** lead tagging and attribution disputes, inventory and price
distribution to dozens of CPs, brokerage calculation and payout processing, CP
performance reporting.

### 4.6 Billing / QS engineer — day in the life

- **Who they are:** a civil engineer on the commercial side of construction. Prepares
  and certifies the **RA (Running Account) bills** that pay subcontractors.
- **Their stack:** **Excel** above all, AutoCAD for drawings, measurement books
  (physical or digital), the ERP (Farvision, SAP, Tally at smaller firms).

**A typical day:** joint measurement at site with the subcontractor's representative —
physically measuring completed work (concrete volume, brickwork area, plaster area,
steel weight) and recording it in the **measurement book**; back at the office, computing
quantities from the measurements and from drawings; preparing the **RA bill** — quantities
this period, cumulative quantities, rates from the work order, deductions for advances,
retention, debit notes for materials issued by the company, and statutory deductions
(TDS, GST treatment); reconciling **material issued versus material theoretically
consumed** for the work claimed — a critical anti-leakage check; getting the bill
certified through the project manager; and disputing the subcontractor's claimed
quantities where they disagree.

**Repetitive-work inventory — billing engineer**

| Task | Frequency | Time | Why it's still manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint measurement recording | Continuous | Hours | Physical measurement at site | Site → measurement book |
| **Quantity computation from drawings** | Every bill | Hours-days | Reading quantities off drawings into Excel | Drawings + measurements → quantities |
| **RA bill preparation** | Monthly, per subcontractor | 2-8 hours each | Excel with rates, deductions, cumulative tracking | Quantities → certified bill |
| **Material reconciliation** | Monthly | Hours | Comparing issued material to theoretical consumption | Store records + work done → variance |
| Rate analysis for extra items | Frequent | Hours | New items not in the original work order | Item → derived rate |
| Subcontractor claim verification | Continuous | Hours | Adversarial checking of claimed quantities | Claim → certified or reduced |

### 4.7 Liaison officer — day in the life

The role has no clean US equivalent. The liaison officer obtains the project's statutory
approvals: plan sanction, commencement certificate, fire NOC, environmental clearance,
utility connections, and finally the occupancy certificate.

**The day is physical and relational:** visiting municipal and departmental offices,
submitting applications and supporting drawings, tracking file movement between desks,
responding to queries and objections, resubmitting corrected drawings, arranging
inspections, and following up — repeatedly and in person — on files that have gone quiet.

**Be precise about what the work is.** It is genuine expertise: knowing which department
needs which document in which format, what objections a particular office typically
raises, what sequence of approvals unblocks the next, and how to keep a file moving.
Delay is the norm and the liaison officer's value is measured in weeks saved.

**Repetitive work:** application and document set assembly (the same drawings and
certificates packaged differently for each department), file status tracking across many
departments with no shared system, objection-response cycles, and approval-expiry
tracking. **The document assembly and status tracking are automatable; the physical
presence and relationships are not.**

### 4.8 Society / facility manager — day in the life

- **Who they are:** manages a gated community of 200-2,000 flats, employed by the
  association or an FM company, working alongside unpaid office-bearers.
- **Their stack:** **MyGate or NoBrokerHood or ADDA**, Tally or Excel for accounts,
  WhatsApp groups (several — the committee group, the residents' group, the staff group),
  a complaint register.

**A typical day:** staff attendance and deployment (security, housekeeping, gardeners,
technicians); the complaint queue — water pressure, lift breakdown, parking disputes,
leakage from the flat above, noise; vendor coordination (lift AMC, DG set service, water
tanker, pest control, STP maintenance); **maintenance billing** and, monthly,
**defaulter follow-up**; society accounts and statutory compliance; and, in season, AGM
preparation — notices, agenda, audited accounts, budget, proxies, minutes.

**Repetitive-work inventory — society management**

| Task | Frequency | Why manual | Data in → data out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maintenance invoice generation** | Monthly, per flat | Area-based or flat rates, arrears, interest, sinking fund | Flat data → invoice |
| **Defaulter identification and follow-up** | Monthly | Repeated reminders to neighbours — socially awkward, which is why it is deferred | Ledger → reminders |
| Complaint intake and routing | Daily | Arrives by WhatsApp, phone, app and in person | Complaint → assigned task |
| Vendor coordination and AMC tracking | Continuous | Contract renewal dates, service schedules | Contract → scheduled service |
| Staff attendance and payroll | Monthly | Manual registers in many societies | Attendance → payroll |
| **AGM administration** | Annual | Notices, quorum, proxies, minutes, resolutions | Meeting → compliant record |
| Statutory filings and audit | Annual | Cooperative society compliance | Books → filings |

**Why this is interesting:** the work is repetitive, the pain is felt directly by the
person who would adopt a tool, the incumbent solutions (MyGate, ADDA) focus on access and
communication more than on billing and administration, and there are a very large number
of societies. The constraint is willingness to pay — societies are cost-conscious and
decisions are made by committee.

### 4.9 India-based analyst doing offshore US real estate work — day in the life

**Read this section carefully. It is the most strategically important one in the chapter
for an India-based builder.**

- **Who they are:** a graduate — often commerce, engineering or law — working a night
  shift in Pune, Bengaluru, Noida, Gurugram, Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai or Ahmedabad,
  performing back-office real estate work for US or UK clients. Employed either by a
  BPO/KPO (Firstsource, Sutherland, WNS, Infosys BPM, Visionet, Altisource and many
  smaller specialists) or by a **captive Global Capability Centre** of a global real
  estate firm or fund administrator.
- **Cost:** typically **Rs 3-15 lakh per year** depending on function and experience.
  Compare to the US equivalents in chapters 03, 04, 06 and 08.

**What they actually do**, by function:

| Function | The work | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| **US title search** | Search a US county's online records (or a title plant), build the chain of title, pull deeds, mortgages, liens, judgments and tax records, and type an abstract or search report on a template | 04 |
| **Mortgage processing support** | Index and classify loan documents, order verifications, prepare condition checklists, perform data entry into the LOS, run initial reviews | 03 |
| **Lease abstraction** | Read a commercial lease and its amendments and extract 80-150 fields into the client's template | 06, 09 |
| **Fund accounting** | Property trial balance loading, reconciliation, capital account allocation, report preparation | 08 |
| **Valuation support** | Comp research, Argus model input, report drafting | 05, 06 |
| **CRE research** | Market data collection, comp verification, report production | 06 |
| **CAD and space planning** | Drafting, floor plan updates, space data maintenance | 09 |
| **FM help desk** | Work order intake and dispatch for a US or European portfolio | 09 |

**A typical night shift:** log in around 6:30-8:30pm IST to overlap with the US business
day; take a queue of files from a workflow tool; work each to a **documented process
(an SOP) with a defined turnaround time and a quality score**; escalate exceptions to a
team lead or the onshore team; hit a per-shift volume target; and be sampled by a quality
team that scores accuracy against the SOP.

**The three characteristics that matter to you:**

1. **The work is already documented as an SOP with a quality standard.** Someone has
   already decomposed the workflow into steps precise enough for a person with no
   domain background to execute. **That is an extraordinarily good starting point for
   building an agent** — the hardest part of automating knowledge work, specifying it
   exactly, has been done and is written down.
2. **The volume and the quality metrics already exist.** You can measure an agent
   against a real, current baseline rather than guessing.
3. **The buyer is reachable from India.** These operations are run by people in Indian
   cities who are measured on cost per file and turnaround time, and who are already
   comfortable with process change.

**The honest counterweight:** the same characteristics mean the market is efficient
about cost. An agent must beat Rs 3-15 lakh per year per analyst *including* the
exception handling, the client's audit requirements, and the risk of an error in a
US-regulated workflow. **Compete on turnaround time, consistency, coverage and
elasticity** — the ability to absorb a volume spike without hiring — rather than purely
on cost.

### 4.10 Bank panel advocate — day in the life

Engaged by banks and HFCs to produce a **title search report** on properties being
mortgaged. Paid **per file, Rs 1,500-5,000**, and works on volume.

The day: receive a batch of files with property documents; for each, search the
sub-registrar's records for the encumbrance certificate; examine the chain of title
across the search period; check the khata/patta, tax receipts, approvals, and OC;
identify defects; and produce a **standard-format opinion** stating whether the title is
clear and marketable and what conditions apply. **The report format is largely fixed and
the analysis is repetitive across files** — but the judgment about whether a defect is
fatal is real legal work, and the advocate's opinion carries professional liability.

Note the duplication highlighted in chapter 13: this same search will be performed again
by the buyer's own advocate, and again by the next buyer's advocate at the next sale.

### 4.11 Home loan DSA — day in the life

A **Direct Selling Agent** sources home loan customers for banks and HFCs on commission
(**roughly 0.3-0.8% of the loan amount**, varying by lender and product). The day:
generating leads through builders, brokers and referrals; collecting the borrower's
document set (identity, address, income, bank statements, property papers); assessing
which lender is most likely to approve at the best rate given the profile; submitting
to multiple lenders; **chasing the lender's credit team through the file's progress**;
coordinating the legal and technical valuation visits; and following up to disbursement,
because **the commission is paid on disbursement, not application**.

**Repetitive work:** document collection and completeness checking, multi-lender
submission of the same file, status chasing across lenders, and disbursement follow-up.
It is the Indian analogue of the loan officer assistant's work in chapter 03, on
commission and without a system of record.

## 5. The WhatsApp and vernacular layer

**Treat this as a first-class technical constraint, not colour.** It is the single
biggest difference between building real estate software for India and for the US.

### How deals actually move

- **Broker groups.** A broker belongs to a dozen or more WhatsApp groups organised by
  locality and asset type. Requirements and inventory are posted as free text, as
  photographs of printed listings, as forwarded PDFs, and as voice notes. Matching
  happens in a human's head.
- **Developer to channel partner.** Inventory lists, price sheets, offer circulars and
  booking updates are distributed by WhatsApp broadcast, usually as PDF or image.
- **Salesperson to customer.** Brochures, cost sheets (frequently a **screenshot of an
  Excel sheet**), payment plans, construction progress photographs, demand letters and
  receipts.
- **Society management.** Residents' groups, committee groups, staff groups; complaints,
  notices and disputes all flow through them.
- **Site coordination.** Progress photographs, material requirements, and instructions
  between site and head office.

### What this means technically

| Reality | Implication |
|---|---|
| The system of record is a chat log | There is no structured data to integrate with. **Your input is unstructured text, images of documents, and audio** |
| Documents arrive as photographs of paper or screenshots | OCR quality is variable; images are skewed, shadowed, and cropped |
| **Voice notes are a primary medium** | Speech recognition across Indian languages, with heavy code-mixing, on poor audio |
| **Code-mixing is the norm** | A single sentence may mix Hindi and English ("Sir, 3BHK ka rate kya hai, and possession kab hai?"). Monolingual models handle this badly |
| Many languages | Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia and more — with regional variation within each |
| No onboarding tolerance | A broker will not install an app or create an account. **Anything requiring a login loses most of the informal market** |
| WhatsApp Business API is the practical channel | Which brings its own template-message rules, opt-in requirements, and per-conversation pricing |

**The design conclusion:** for the informal half of Indian real estate, the winning
interface is probably **WhatsApp itself**, in the user's own language, accepting
photographs and voice notes as input and returning short, actionable text. That is a
genuinely different product from a web dashboard, and it plays directly to what current
multilingual and multimodal models are good at.

**But verify the regulatory ground before building outbound.** India's telecom regulator
maintains a framework for commercial communications, unsolicited commercial
communication, and consent — and it has been tightened repeatedly. Business messaging on
WhatsApp is additionally governed by the platform's own opt-in and template rules.
Inbound and consented interactions are far safer ground than outbound campaigns, exactly
as in the US (chapter 11), though the specific rules differ.

## 6. The cost baseline and what it means for automation

Here is the argument in full, because getting it wrong is the most likely way to waste a
year building the wrong Indian product.

**The claim:** in India, an AI agent competes against labour costing **Rs 15,000-60,000
per month** for most of the repetitive roles in this chapter. A US processor or
coordinator costs 15-40 times that. **A cost-savings pitch that works in the US does not
automatically work in India.**

**Where that leaves a real opportunity, in order of strength:**

1. **Speed and coverage.** A caller works nine hours; an agent responds in seconds at
   any hour. In a market where speed-to-lead determines conversion and site visits are
   booked on weekends, response latency is worth real money.
2. **Consistency and data quality.** Human CRM disposition in Indian pre-sales is
   notoriously poor because it is done in a rush at day's end. An agent that captures
   every interaction accurately makes the *rest of the funnel* work — which is worth
   more than the caller's salary.
3. **Attrition.** High turnover in tele-calling and post-sales means constant hiring and
   retraining. Removing that cost and quality variance is a distinct argument from
   removing the salary.
4. **Compliance reliability.** RERA QPR filings, demand-letter accuracy, TDS compliance,
   society statutory filings — where the failure mode is a penalty rather than an
   inefficiency, buyers pay for reliability.
5. **Elasticity.** A launch weekend needs ten times the normal calling capacity for
   three days. Hiring cannot flex like that.
6. **Serving the offshore export business** (section 4.9), where the client is paying in
   dollars and measuring turnaround, so the economics resemble the US case.

**Where the argument might be wrong, stated honestly:** if Indian labour costs rise
materially, or if a single agent can credibly replace several roles at once rather than
augmenting one, the pure cost case improves. And in the offshore-services segment the
cost case is already strong because revenue is priced in hard currency against US labour
benchmarks. **Do not over-generalise the "labour is cheap in India" point — it applies to
the domestic market, not to the export market, and those are two different businesses
sitting in the same buildings.**

## 7. Where the human genuinely adds value

- **In-person negotiation across a cultural and educational gap**, in the buyer's own
  language, often with an extended family present.
- **Trust in a low-trust transaction.** Buyers are risking their life savings on an
  unbuilt flat from a developer they cannot verify. Reassurance is the product.
- **Local knowledge that exists nowhere else** — which builder delivers, which layout
  has approval problems, which society is well-run, which stretch floods.
- **Physical presence** at the sub-registrar office, at site visits, at inspections, at
  departmental offices.
- **Relationships with officials and institutions** that determine how fast a file moves.
- **Legal judgment on defective title** on incomplete records.
- **Handling distress** — a buyer whose project has stalled for four years is not having
  a customer service interaction.

## 8. Glossary

- **AGM** — Annual General Meeting of a housing society or association.
- **AMC (contract)** — Annual Maintenance Contract, e.g. for lifts or DG sets.
- **Bayana / token** — Earnest money.
- **BHK** — Bedroom-Hall-Kitchen; the Indian unit configuration notation (2BHK, 3BHK).
- **Channel partner (CP)** — Broker firm selling a developer's inventory for developer-paid commission.
- **Cost sheet** — The itemised total cost of a unit including base price, charges, taxes and duties.
- **CRM (Indian developer usage)** — The **post-sales** function: demands, collections, documentation, possession.
- **Demand letter** — The developer's notice that a construction-linked payment is now due.
- **DSA** — Direct Selling Agent; commission-based loan sourcing.
- **GCC** — Global Capability Centre; a multinational's captive offshore operation in India.
- **Liaison officer** — The person who obtains statutory approvals from government departments.
- **MB (Measurement Book)** — The record of measured work at a construction site.
- **MIS** — Management Information System; in practice, the recurring Excel report.
- **Possession** — Handover of the completed unit to the buyer.
- **Pre-sales** — The tele-calling function that qualifies leads and books site visits.
- **RA bill** — Running Account bill; a subcontractor's periodic payment claim.
- **RM** — Relationship Manager.
- **Site visit** — The prospect's visit to the project; the pivotal conversion event.
- **Society** — Cooperative housing society or apartment owners' association.
- **Thekedar** — Labour contractor supplying and managing construction crews.
