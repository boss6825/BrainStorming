# Stigmergy and memory in LLM agent swarms: a literature map

Stigmergy — coordination through traces left in a shared environment rather than direct messaging — and persistent memory are converging on the same design question in LLM-driven agent swarms: what an agent should *write down* so that other agents (or its later self) act usefully on it. The field has no settled canon yet; instead, four older streams are colliding in a wave of 2025–2026 preprints, and the most interesting recent results are cautionary — expanded memory and larger collectives can *degrade* coordination rather than improve it. This map traces those four streams, shows where they meet, and names the open problems a builder of stigmergic, memory-bearing LLM swarms will hit.

## The foundational idea: coordination without messages

The term stigmergy was coined by Pierre-Paul Grassé to explain how termites build without a blueprint or direct communication: each worker's action modifies the environment, and that modified environment cues the next action. [Theraulaz and Bonabeau](https://doi.org/10.1162/106454699568700) give the canonical synthesis, distinguishing *sematectonic* stigmergy (the work-in-progress itself is the signal) from *sign-based* stigmergy (a dedicated marker, like a pheromone, carries the signal), a distinction that maps cleanly onto LLM-agent designs where the shared artifact is either the task product itself or a separate annotation layer. The mechanism's defining property is that coordination is *indirect, asynchronous, and local*: agents need not be co-present, addressable, or even aware of each other, only of the trace.

That property made stigmergy the engine of swarm intelligence's most durable algorithms. Ant Colony Optimization, introduced as the Ant System by [Dorigo, Maniezzo and Colorni](https://doi.org/10.1109/3477.484436) and later formalised as a metaheuristic by [Dorigo, Birattari and Stützle](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/1290.003.0004), is pure sign-based stigmergy: artificial ants deposit pheromone on graph edges proportional to solution quality, the pheromone evaporates over time, and the colony converges on short paths without any ant holding a global plan. [AntNet](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.530) applied the same loop to adaptive network routing, demonstrating that a stigmergic trace can act as a distributed, self-repairing memory of network state. The closely related Particle Swarm Optimization of [Kennedy and Eberhart](https://doi.org/10.1109/icnn.1995.488968) shares the collective-search framing though it coordinates through shared best-position values rather than an environmental deposit. Two design primitives recur across all of this and transfer directly to LLM swarms: a *deposit-and-evaporate* dynamic that lets stale traces decay, and *positive feedback with bounded reinforcement* that converges the collective without freezing it. The swarm-robotics lineage that grew from this work, surveyed by [Dorigo, Theraulaz and Trianni](https://doi.org/10.1109/jproc.2021.3072740), is the empirical proving ground for what indirect coordination can and cannot achieve at scale.

## The learning-era bridge

Before LLMs, a small body of work already fused stigmergy with learning agents, and it prefigures today's questions. [Xu and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1811.08210) proposed brain-inspired stigmergy learning, using a stigmergic medium as the substrate for distributed control and arguing the mechanism is well suited to large-scale swarm problems precisely because it is robust and adaptive. The same group's [Stigmergic Independent Reinforcement Learning](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1911.12504) let independently-learning agents coordinate through an environmental medium rather than a shared reward or communication channel, the direct ancestor of the question of whether LLM agents can coordinate through a shared scratchpad without explicit message passing. On the systems side, [stigLD](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-19-1253-5_13) grounded stigmergic coordination in Linked Data, making the shared environment a structured, queryable web resource; this is an early answer to *what the trace medium should be* that anticipates today's shared-memory stores. These papers establish that stigmergy and individual learning are composable; the open question they leave is what changes when each agent is a language model with its own context window and its own memory.

## LLM multi-agent systems

The current generation of LLM agents arrived oriented around direct communication, not stigmergy. The single-agent substrate is [ReAct](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.03629), which interleaves reasoning traces with actions, and [Reflexion](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2303.11366), which adds verbal self-feedback stored in an episodic buffer; it was the first widely-adopted "memory" in an LLM agent, and notably a *private* one. The multi-agent frameworks built on top mostly coordinate by conversation: [CAMEL](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2303.17760) through structured role-play dialogue, [AutoGen](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.08155) through configurable conversational agents, and [MetaGPT](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.00352) and [ChatDev](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.07924) through software-company metaphors where agents pass artifacts down a pipeline. MetaGPT is the most stigmergic of these in practice: it enforces a *shared message pool* and a structured-document publishing convention, so agents subscribe to and act on artifacts in a common space rather than addressing each other, environment-mediated coordination in all but name, even though the paper does not frame it that way. The standout for emergent social behaviour is [Generative Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763), whose simulated townspeople coordinate partly through a shared world they observe and modify, and partly through a memory stream with retrieval, reflection, and planning, making it the clearest early demonstration that environmental traces plus individual memory produce believable collective behaviour. [Voyager](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.16291) extends the memory angle differently, accumulating a reusable skill library that functions as a persistent, additive store of learned competence. The surveys by [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11704-024-40231-1) and [Xi et al.](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.07864) map this landscape, and both treat memory as a core architectural axis while treating stigmergic coordination as, at most, an implicit special case.

## Memory: private buffer to shared store

Memory is where the two streams fuse, and the architecture has matured fast. [MemGPT](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2310.08560) reframed the context window as virtual memory with OS-style paging between a small in-context working set and a large external store, giving a single agent effectively unbounded recall, the dominant pattern for *individual* long-term memory. [Cognitive Architectures for Language Agents (CoALA)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.02427) supplies the vocabulary the field now uses, factoring agent memory into working, episodic, semantic, and procedural stores and positioning retrieval and learning as the operations over them. The [memory-mechanism survey by Zhang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3748302) is the most thorough consolidation, taxonomising how agents write, manage, and read memory and flagging cross-agent and lifelong memory as open frontiers. The conceptual leap relevant to stigmergy is the move from *private* memory (Reflexion's buffer, MemGPT's store) to *shared* memory: once multiple agents read and write a common persistent store, that store *is* a stigmergic medium, and the design questions become the classic stigmergic ones — what to deposit, how it decays, and how to keep positive feedback from running away.

## The frontier: stigmergy meets memory

The 2025–2026 preprints are the first to name the intersection directly, and several are warnings rather than wins. [Ledger-State Stigmergy](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.03997) is the cleanest formalisation: it models autonomous on-chain agents that coordinate by reading and writing shared ledger state instead of exchanging messages, giving indirect coordination a rigorous, auditable substrate and making explicit that blockchain state is a stigmergic trace. [SwarmSys](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.10047) builds a decentralised, closed-loop LLM swarm that coordinates through environmental signals rather than fixed roles or central control, reporting better scalability on long-horizon reasoning, a direct test of stigmergic principles in an LLM collective. [SuperBrain](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.00510) and the [dynamic-orchestration work of Zhang et al.](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.08009) pursue the same goal of automating swarm coordination so that collective structure emerges rather than being hand-specified, and the [stigmergic subgraph-isomorphism solver](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.02449) shows the classical pheromone loop being ported wholesale onto agent-based graph search.

The cautionary results are the more important contribution for a researcher designing memory mechanisms. [The Memory Curse](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.08060) reports that across seven LLMs and four social-dilemma games over 500 rounds, *expanding* the accessible history degraded cooperation in most model–game settings; more memory made the collective worse, not better, which directly contradicts the assumption that larger context is a free upgrade. [When Agents Misremember Collectively](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.00428) documents a "Mandela effect" in which shared memory propagates a collective false belief through a multi-agent system, a stigmergic failure mode where a corrupted trace becomes self-reinforcing. [The Bystander Effect in Multi-Agent Reasoning](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.10698) shows simulated social pressure inducing "cognitive loafing," where agents in a collective contribute less than they would alone, an emergent coordination pathology with no analogue in classical insect swarms. Against these, [Governed Collaborative Memory](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.04264) is the constructive response: it treats durable, reloadable, behaviour-shaping shared memory as something that must be *governed*, curated by a selection process analogous to artificial selection, precisely because, left ungoverned, shared traces drift, corrupt, and amplify noise. Read together, this cluster says the deposit-and-evaporate and bounded-reinforcement primitives from ACO are not optional niceties but the missing safeguards that keep a shared LLM memory from becoming the Memory Curse.

## What is established, contested, and open

Three things are reasonably established. First, indirect coordination through a shared environment is sufficient for non-trivial collective problem-solving, settled by decades of ACO and swarm robotics and is now being reproduced in LLM swarms by SwarmSys and the ledger-state formalism. Second, the working vocabulary for agent memory (working/episodic/semantic/procedural, with explicit write–manage–read operations) is stable and widely adopted via CoALA and the Zhang survey. Third, shared persistent memory and a stigmergic medium are the *same object* viewed from two traditions, which means the swarm-intelligence toolkit — decay, bounded positive feedback, quality-weighted deposition — is directly applicable to LLM memory design.

What is contested is whether more memory helps. The Memory Curse and the bystander/Mandela results are recent, mostly single-group preprints, and they cut against the prevailing "bigger context is better" intuition; they need replication, but they are specific and measured enough to take seriously as a research target rather than dismiss. Also unresolved is the right *medium* for the trace — natural-language scratchpads, structured stores (the stigLD / Linked Data line), or formal ledger state each make different trade-offs between expressiveness and the ability to apply decay and conflict resolution.

The open problems most relevant to a researcher building stigmergic, memory-bearing LLM swarms are concrete. There is no accepted *evaporation* mechanism for natural-language traces: text does not decay the way pheromone does, and nobody has shown a principled way to age or forget shared entries so that stale coordination signals stop driving behaviour. There is no standard benchmark that isolates stigmergic coordination from direct messaging, so claims about emergent coordination are hard to compare. The failure modes (false-belief propagation, loafing, reward-like runaway in shared memory) are documented but not yet mapped to the design parameters that cause them. And the governance question: who curates the shared store, by what selection rule, at what cadence, is named by exactly one preprint and otherwise wide open. These are the gaps where the swarm-intelligence literature has answers that the LLM-agent literature has not yet imported.
